**Executive Summary**

Davao City is a highly urbanized city in Mindanao. It is the largest city in the world in terms of land area. The city has eight (8) watersheds and six(6) river systems bounded by different provinces. It is one of the most livable cities in the World.

With all its distinctions, the City has not been exempted from both natural and human-induced disasters as it has experienced in the past. Despite all these experiences, the City was able to provide the needed intervention to restore the communities that were victims of haphazard disasters that destroy both lives and properties of the people.

**Development Policies and Initiatives**

The passage of the City's Comprehensive Land-Used Plan (CLUP) has provided a new dimension in terms of relocating people in areas that are far from the existing hazards in the City. These development policies will ensure that future developments will now be located away from the hazards.

Likewise, environmental policies enacted by the legislative body of the City, once again proves that the City is serious in protecting the remaining natural assets that could be the City's obligation to the next generations.

**Building Back Better Principle**

The building back better principle is geared towards a sustainable development in the City that would provide safer and more resilient infrastructures that could withstand disasters. This principle will provide more adaptive communities and sustainable development.

**Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Plan**

It is on this aspect that the City shall be responsible in crafting the Davao City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Plan to answer the growing needs of formulating and aligning the thrust of the Local Government in addressing the DRR programs and projects of the City. This plan will ultimately be the basis for providing for a livable City for all.

**Thematic Area 1: Disaster Prevention and Mitigation**

Disaster Prevention and Mitigation provides key strategic actions to activities revolving around hazards assessment and mitigation, vulnerability analysis, identification of hazard-prone areas and mainstreets, and DRRM into development plans. It underscores the need for sound and scientific analysis of the different underlying vulnerability factors, risks and exposure to hazards and disasters.

**Thematic Area 2: Disaster Preparedness**

Page 4

--- [Page 9] ---
Disaster Preparedness provides for key strategic actions that give importance to community awareness and understanding, contingency planning, conduct of local drills, and the development of a Davao Disaster Response Plan. In preparing communities and governments for possible disasters, predetermined needs based on information available are crucial. Risk-related communication coming from the Prevention and Mitigation aspect is necessary in order for disaster response activities to be responsive to the needs of affected communities. Also, policies, budget and institutional mechanisms established under the Disaster Prevention and Mitigation aspect will be further enhanced through capacity building activities, development of coordination mechanisms, among others. Through these, coordination, complementation and interoperability of work in DRRM operations and essential services will be ensured.

It should be noted that the activities in this thematic area are not linear but cyclic and anticipate improvements over time. Behavioral change created by the Preparedness aspect is eventually measured by how well people respond to disasters. At the frontlines of disaster preparedness are local government units and communities. Overall, the bottom line is having more prepared citizens and governments.

Thematic Area 3. Disaster Response

Disaster Response provides for key actions that give importance to activities during the actual disaster response operations such as needs assessment, search and rescue, relief operations, and early recovery activities. The activities identified below will be done either before the actual response operations or during the disaster event. For those activities which need to be completed prior to actual response operations, they will be linked to activities earlier identified in the preparedness aspect. However, to ensure that the proper response "fear" is issued in doing the said activities, they have been included in this aspect. Overall, the success and realization of this priority area rely heavily on the completion of the activities under both the prevention and mitigation and preparedness aspects.

This aspect includes Early Recovery which means, under IRR Rule 2 Section 1, the multidimensional process of recovery that begins in a humanitarian crisis and is guided by development principles that seek to build on humanitarian programs and catalyze sustainable development opportunities. It aims to generate self-sufficiency and community-owned, resilient processes for post-crisis recovery. Early recovery encompasses the restoration of basic services, livelihoods, governance, security, and rule of law, environment and social dimensions, including reintegration and social and emotional rehabilitation of displaced populations.

Thematic Area 4. Disaster Rehabilitation and Recovery

The Disaster Rehabilitation and Recovery aspect covers concerns related to employment and livelihoods, infrastructure and lifeline facilities, and housing and resettlement. These are recovery efforts done when people are already outside of the evacuation center.

I. DAVAO CITY PROFILE

Background:
Davao City is considered one of the largest cities in the world in terms of land area. It has an expanse of more than 244,000 hectares and is populated by about 1.5 million. It has one hundred eighty-two (182) barangays spread throughout the eleven (11) administrative districts. It is located at the south-eastern part of Mindanao Island, with the Davao Gulf to its north; with the Davao del Norte Province in the north, with Davao del Norte Province in the northeast; with Bukidnon Province in the north, with Davao del Sur in the south; it is bounded in the east by the Davao Gulf. It is also the biggest urban market in the growth polygon area called BIMP-EAGA(Brunel-Indonesia-Malaysia-Philippines East Asian Growth Area).

Of the one-hundred eighty-two (182) barangays, ninety-two(92) has been categorized as rural, and the remaining ninety is considered urban barangays.

Demography
Davao City, a highly urbanized City in Region XI, posted a total population of 1,449,296 persons as of 2010 national census, an increase of 85,959 persons over its total population of 1,363,337 persons in 2007. An increase in the population from 2007 to 2010 translated to an average population growth rate (PGR) of 1.35%, lower than the 2.44% average annual PGR for the city for the period 2000 to 2007.

Topography

A substantial part of Davao City is mountainous, characterized by extensive mountain ranges with uneven distribution of plateaus and lowlands. The mountain range which defines the western boundary of the city extends as far down to South Cotabato. This mountain range nurtures the highest peak in the Philippines, which is Mt. Apo located at the boundaries of North Cotabato, Davao del Sur and Davao City. Mt. Apo has an elevation of about 10,131 feet (3,144 meters) above sea level. It has been considered as a semi-active volcano.

The large, contiguous lowland areas of Davao City are coastal plains and valleys extending inland as gently-rising valleys. These areas are found in the eastern part of the city, in Pantar and Bantayan, which is an extensive plain. Other lowland areas are found in the south eastern part of the city along the western coast of the Gulf, and are in the districts of Bunawan, Buhangan, Agdao, Poblacion, Talomo, and Toril. These lowland areas are level to nearly level with slopes ranging from 0 to 3 percent. These are generally composed of recent alluvium consisting of clay, silt and some sand and gravel.

The entire area of Davao City is drained towards the Gulf Davao River and its numerous tributaries. The main drainage system of the city, Davao River, originates from Bukidnon Province, flows towards the south meandering along the central part and finally flows eastward emptying into the Gulf at the southern periphery of the City Proper. The secondary drainage system of the city is the Talomo River which is the drainage outlets of the eastern slopes of Mt. Apo. Other major drainage system includes the Lipadás River, Matina River, Lasang River and Sibulan River.

Hydrography

Davao City lies within the eight watersheds, namely: Tugnanay watershed, Lasang Watershed, Bunawan watershed, Davao River watershed, Pangi watershed, Talomo watershed, Lipadás watershed and the Sibulan watershed. These rivers run crisscross towards the almost 54-kilometer stretch of shoreline to the Davao Gulf.

The southernmost is the Sibulan Watershed which has its headwaters in Davao City and draining towards the municipality of Sta. Cruz. Lipadás River Watershed cuts across from the slopes of Mt. Apo draining towards Lizaoda. Talomo Watershed has its headwaters in Mt. Talomo traversing parts of Calinan and Tugbbox Districts flowing down to Barangay Talomo. The smallest major watershed is the Matina Pangi River Watershed which covers parts of Brgy. Matina, Matina Pangi and Matina River. The largest is the Davao River Watershed which ironically has its headwaters in Bukidnon, covering parts of Marilog and Paquelibo districts funneling down towards Poblacion District. Bunawan watershed cover river that flow from Paquelibo district entering Davao del Norte and draining back to Bunawan district. Tugnanay River Watershed is almost totally part of Davao del Norte except the middle portion that enters Paquelibo District and drained towards Lasang River.

**Weather**

Davao City enjoys a tropical climate. Compared with other parts of the Philippines in which there is a distinct hot and wet season, it enjoys the privilege of a climate where the days are always sunny and mild followed by nights of rain. The city is outside the typhoon belt and lacks major seasonal variations.

From 1990 to 2010, the average annual temperature varied from 34.2°C (highest) in April to 21.43°C (lowest) in January. The average annual rainfall in 1990 was recorded at 140 mm and reached 150 mm in 2010 as recorded in two rain stations in Bago Obrero and Davao International Airport.

The predominant wind direction is northward from the Davao Gulf where the cooler air of the sea replaces the warm air mass over the city. A surrounding chain of mountains protects the city effectively from strong winds.

The city does not have a pronounced wet or dry season, but has a rainfall that is evenly distributed throughout the year. The city experiences heavy downpours but is practically typhoon-free. However, in December 4, 2012, the City has partly experienced the effects brought by Typhoon Pablo (Bopha).

**Climate Change**

As stated in the UN International Strategy for Disaster Reduction (ISDR) Policy Issues and Guidelines, water-related disasters account for 90 percent of all natural disasters, and their frequency and intensity is generally rising.

Based on the projections made by PAGASA, seasonal rainfall is expected to decrease in the next 50 years but there is an expected significant increase for the months of December to February. As a direct effect, floods are expected to be more severe. These projections also indicate increases in days with temperatures ranging 15°C and above as well as the number of dry days.

**Hazard Profile**

The dominant geomorphological processes acting per terrain mapping unit were identified and evaluated by the MGB. This is very useful in determining the potential geo-hazards in the city.

**Flooding -** Areas naturally prone to flooding are all the fluvial landforms. However, detailed analysis and study is needed to further subdivide the fluvial landform into various susceptibility and vulnerability classes. The areas delineated with high susceptibility to flooding correspond to approximately 30-50 year floods. Flooding occurring outside the delineated flood prone areas could be mainly man-induced resulting to excessive overland flow or runoff.

**Mass Movement -** At least four (4) factors (soil class, slope, presence of landslide and landslide potential) were considered in the delineation of the landslide susceptibility classes. Areas which are naturally susceptible to mass movements are the denudational landforms. However, based on landslide mapping and inventory mass movements were also noted in other areas that are moderately deep to deeply incised valleys of the volcanic landform, the structural-denudational landform and the karst landform.

**Erosion -** Generally, the rate of erosion is controlled by slope, climate, and natural of bedrock vegetation cover and human intervention. The delineated erosion susceptibility classes for the study area considered only the natural factors particularly the slope, soil and underlying lithology. The present land use and cultivation/tilling practices could also result in induced erosion.

**Earthquake-induced hazards (seismicity, liquefaction)** - Liquefaction is a geo-hazard that results from seismicity/earthquake and defined as the granular material in solid state, which behaves and deforms like a liquid as a consequence of increased pore water pressure due to earthquake shaking. Generally, areas belonging to the fluvial landform are the most susceptible to liquefaction being overlain by alluvial deposits wherein the same conditions mentioned above could be present.

**Volcanic -** Mts. Sibulan, Talomo and Apo are volcanic complex. The Sibulan-Talomo volcanic complex was previously classified as non-active cones; therefore preparation of a volcanic hazard map at this time is not necessary. Although Phivolcs consider Mt. Apo as potentially active, this has to be further studied.

II. RISK PROFILE OF DAVAO CITY

Like any other area in the Philippines, Davao City is not exempted both from man-made or human induced and natural disasters.

In the past the City has been the recipient of different disasters.

From data available, the top three (3) calamities that hit Davao City repeatedly since 1996 to date are: 1) Fires and Conflagrations; 2) Floods; and 3) Sea Swells, in that order.

Noteworthy also were the terrorist-initiated incidents of bombing that have caused death and injury to unarmed civilians.

1. Fires & Conflagrations

Through the years, Davao City has progressed and developed. But while progress and development are bustling as demonstrated by the construction of high-rise buildings and areas of the art facilities and amenities while certain residential districts in the downtown area remain to be rustic and still maintain an old setting and packed with structures that are made of combustible materials that are highly susceptible to conflagration. Without mentioning, many of the residential areas in the downtown are thickly populated.

2. Floods and Flooding Incidents

The general characteristics of the geographical/topographical and meteorological conditions are not rigid and dogmatic indicators that Davao City is invulnerable to natural calamities. There are other factors which indirectly affect the meteorological conditions. There had been recent events in the past when intense and prolonged rainfall brought about by rain-producing phenomena - low pressure areas, tropical cyclones, etc. occurred. These phenomena can cause flooding due to overflows and flooding. The areas along the riverbanks are relatively flat and susceptible to flooding, especially when other astronomical phenomena such as high tide coincide with the occurrence of heavy and prolonged rainfall.

The June 28-29, 2011 flooding in Talomo District and the January 19-20 along the Davao River System has reversed the level of awareness of the people in the flooded areas. Aside from the direct damages that flooding events can cause, there are a host of other calamities that may occur as an aftermath of a flood. Interruption of daily activities that result to the lack of basic necessities such as water, food, and shelter, and the disruption of the sanitary regimen of a calamity-stricken community. These invariably can lead to an outbreak of gastro-intestinal and other diseases.

3. Monsoon Wave/Sea Swells

However, sea swells are practically a yearly occurrence. These occurrences are caused by southwest monsoon and usually happen during the second and third quarter of each year. From 1996 to date, collated data on sea swell occurrences indicate coastaline communities are hit annually.

Davao City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Plan Page 33

--- [Page 38] ---
4. Rain-induced Landslides

Based on data available, the City has not also escaped from the occurrences of landslides. At present, several areas in the City have been susceptible to landslides due to the topography of the City. Many of these areas had already experienced landslides brought about by continuous downpour.

In the past only barangays classified as rural were exposed to rain-induced landslides, but up to this date some barangays classified as urban is beginning to be exposed to this type of hazard.

5. Other natural hazards

While the city has not experienced any major natural disaster in terms of lives lost and properties damaged in the last 50 years, this should not be reassuring enough for the residents of the city and the government to be complacent and not to plan for worst-case scenarios. The geographical location and the history of the city makes it potentially susceptible to other geo-locations and natural calamities such as earthquakes and its resultant hazards such as ground shaking, landslide, liquefaction, ground rupture and worst of all, tsunamis. There have been incidents that have devastated Davao City although it practically straddles on a fault line. There also had been incidents of soil erosion that have caused damage to crops and other agricultural products.

On the other hand, with Climate Change, sea level rise, drought and other associated hazard could also be a threat for the City.

6. Bombings

Finally, the city does not only have its fair share of natural calamities. It has also been a hapless victim to acts of terrorism. Bombing, hostage-taking, kidnap-for-ransom incidents are examples of acts of terrorism that have transpired in the city in the past and just recently this year. Bombings particularly have left scars not only on the victims who survived but also to the psychological make-up of the survivors. The San Pedrito Church bombings in April 1981, and in December 1993, the San Sebastian Church bombing in March 2003, the bombing at the Jasa Puri in April 4, 2003 bombing outside the gate of the Jasa Puri and the February 24, 2005 bombing at the Overland Transport Terminal are vivid reminders of the acts of terrorism that destroys hundreds of lives as a resultant effect of the multi-faceted disaster.

III. DEVELOPMENT POLICIES AND INITIATIVES OF THE CITY

Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Initiatives of the City

The passage of the Comprehensive Land-use Plan (CLUP) of the City is a realization that the Local Government, policy-makers, and other stakeholders are in the forefront of making the City a safe place to live in. It also provides a new perspective in the environmental protection of the City. Several environmental ordinances have been passed by the local legislative body. Worthy to mention is the declaration of making the Shrine Hills as a protected area and the recently passed Comprehensive Anti-Smoking Ordinance of the Davao City.

The reconstitution of the Davao City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council is a real initiative geared towards institutionalizing the DIRR and CCA in the City.

Although, Davao City in 1996 has already created a Disaster Coordinating Division with eight (8) regular ranks and file personnel tasked to implement disaster-related concerns of the City. The passage of the Republic Act has strengthened the capacity of the City to address the growing concern for the protection of all people in the City.

Despite all the initiatives being undertaken by the City, challenges still continue to appear by virtue of the City's massive development brought about by influx of people for purposes of livelihood, education, recreation and or to reside permanently in the City because of its stability in peace and order.

Disasters that happened in the past have provided us a new challenge to face for another milestones in DIRR and CCA. Families that have been displaced by disasters in the past were provided with the chance to recover from the effects brought about by these disasters. Although, the City has initiated a City Shelter Program, many of these families still prefer to stay in areas susceptible to same hazards.

Building Back Better Principle

In the context of post-disaster reconstruction, the "building back better" principle is being promoted. However, current practices have not ensured safer and better structures are built on safe locations during reconstruction and rehabilitation. Neither have engineering and land use practices been risk sensitive such that mitigating future disasters are addressed. Through the combination of increased knowledge and experience, DIRR mainstreaming into development plans and policies, and building institutional mechanisms through monitoring, evaluation and information, "building back better" can be achieved. Over time, improvements in the way DIRR and CCA are addressed in infrastructures, utilities, buildings, and housings should be seen.

--- [Page 40] ---
Building capacities of people and institutions

Gaps in applying resilience principles to actual practice exist at different levels of government, among disaster management practitioners and various stakeholders. Roles and responsibilities need to be understood in the context of emergency situations and DRR. Thus, comprehensive baseline capacity building programs on DRRM and CCA should be developed and implemented in order to be effective and responsive to the needs of people and institutions. These capacity building activities will help enhance their understanding and skills so that DRRM and CCA principles and concepts can be applied, and concrete actions can be taken towards building resilience.

It is guided by development principles that seek to build on humanitarian programmes and catalyze sustainable development opportunities. It also seeks to provide self-sustained nationally-owned, resilient processes for post-crisis recovery. Early recovery encompasses the restoration of basic services, livelihoods, governance, security and rule of law, environment and social dimensions, including reintegration and social and emotional rehabilitation of displaced populations.

Cross-Cutting Concerns

The Davao City DRRMP recognizes that certain concerns cut across the four thematic areas. These include health, human-induced disasters, gender mainstreaming, environmental protection, cultural sensitivity or indigenous practices, and the rights-based approach. The Davao City DRRMP shall be the unified instrument by which the Davao City Government ensures consistency of DRM measures with the physical framework, social and economic and environmental plans of barangays, communities, and other institutions in the City.

Health

People's vulnerability to disaster has become more complicated with the presence of climate change. Hazards such as floods and drought can overlap, resulting in another type of disaster. Minor disturbances in the environment surrounding the ecosystem can have far-reaching consequences on the ecosystems' ability to provide services such as water supply, food production, and air quality. Malaria and dengue (which increases mosquito abundance in areas experiencing warmer and damper temperatures, respectively). Likewise, rising sea-level will increase flooding events disproportionately affect the poor through sanitation of their water sources, and intrusion of sea waters in the different sources of waters. From this viewpoint, the City look into this very critical concerns and incorporated in the Davao City DRRMP.

Human-induced disasters

Since the City has not been exempted by this different human-induced disasters brought about by armed conflict and acts of terrorism, this plan would also look into considering some very important concerns. In conflict affected areas, most people are vulnerable not only because they exposed to natural hazards but more often due to disasters more commonly associated with armed conflict and acts of terrorism such as bomb explosions. Considerations of the factors which contribute to these risks and vulnerabilities should be looked into at all times, taking cognizance of the underlying causes of people's vulnerabilities.

Gender mainstreaming

Gender mainstreaming is about the recognition, acceptance, identification and addressing of the different roles, needs, capacities and vulnerabilities of men and women. The City through Davao City DRMP is committed to promoting gender-sensitive vulnerability and capacity analysis in all disaster risk reduction and management activities. It encourages balancing the roles, responsibilities, needs, interests, capacities of and effect on both genders of response and contingency plans as well as implementation of community-based activities. Gender mainstreaming is about reducing the vulnerabilities of men and women to disasters and encourages a balance in the participation and decision making roles of men and women in DRRM.

Environmental protection

The enactment of different environmental ordinances of the City by the Legislative body has put the City in the forefront of environmental protection. Concerns for the environment is essential component of DRR/CCA in order to minimize vulnerability and avoid increasing the risk of this community. It also underlines the thrust of the Local Government to sustain the ecosystem of the City. Strategic Environmental Areas are established so that every activities in all priority areas do not create stress on the remaining natural resources.

Cultural sensitivity/indigenous practices

Davao City is being inhabited by different Indigenous Peoples. Hence, the Davao City DRMP recognizes the importance of culturally-sensitive risk reduction measures at all levels. People's vulnerability to disasters as well as their capacities to adapt to the changing realities are more often than not related to cultural and indigenous practices. By being sensitive to the indigenous practices and local knowledge, DRRM approaches will become more effective and more easily understood and embraced by the people without any discrimination as to race, religion, culture and ethnicity.

Rights-based approach

Republic Act 10121, has on its policy on peoples rights to life and property. Since people has the right to be protected, this Davao City DRMP will ensure that the policies being laid down will then be provided. This policy has also been the thrust of the City Government of Davao. Priority was given because people have the right to live, safety, information, education, cultural beliefs and right to better lives as protected by the Constitution.

V. IMPLEMENTATION STRATEGIES

Strategies are identified to implement the Davao City DRMMF and achieve the desired key results under each of the four thematic areas. This results may inter-fused with each other but the ultimate aimed is to achieved the desired dream of making Davao City as the most Livable City in Asia.

Advocacy and Information, Education and Communication (IEC)

A strong commitment is required to save lives and properties threatened by natural and human-induced disasters. Achieving this will need increasing visibility and understanding of DRMM and CCA issues, executing and developing more partnerships, encouraging actions and gathering public support for successful implementation of the different activities. The Davao City DRMMF will use evidence-based advocacy to influence people, policies and practices that contribute to the resilience of resilient communities by raising awareness, working with the media and key stakeholders. The Davao City DRMM will continue to develop advocacy, IEC and various communication strategies based on risk assessments and good DRMM practices to be shared with other barangays and communities in the City.

Community-based capability building

The existence of different skilled trainers in the City will now be strengthened to provide a uniform approach and provide a venue for knowledge sharing from different pool of trainers coming from other organizations and institutions in the City.

The Knowledge Management Training Institute will coordinate and becomes the venue for this activity in order to customized training programs and ensure that people are trained based on the needed skills in the different DRMM aspects with out prejudice to given culture and tradition of a certain community. Different people have different needs and capacities and developing community-based capability building programs ensures that knowledge, skills and attitudes are enhanced and built upon further.

Contingency Planning

Plans as an essential component shall be revisited or attuned to the changing times. Contingency and other response plans will now be reviewed and tested to ensure that it will provide a better view of the existing society.

Different thematic areas of the DRMM shall be given importance and aligned with the thrust of the Local Government. Past experiences of disasters shall be taken into consideration for the development and revision of all this plans. Likewise, BDRMMC shall also be encouraged to have their plans in adherence to this plan to ensure uniformity of responses during emergencies and disaster.

--- [Page 69] ---
Education on DRRM and CCA for all

Mainstreaming DRRM into formal education should be undertaken through the integration of DRRM and CCA concepts in the curriculum for basic education, the National Service Training Program (NSTP) at the tertiary level, and bachelor's degree programs. This also includes the conduct of DRRM and CCA training of all public sector employees as mandated by law and the training of teachers and development of teaching materials at the primary and secondary levels.

Institutionalization of DRRMCs and LDRRMOs

Davao City Government has in 1996 created the Disaster Coordinating Division whose mandate is to ensure that all DRRM programs and projects of the City shall be implemented. However with the advent of Republic Act 10211 which requires creation of permanent local DRRM offices to attuned to the new law. It is also ensured that all DRRM-related activities, plans and programs will be implemented and sustained, especially at the local level. The Davao City DRRM Council and Barangay BDRRMCs shall also served as a tool in ensuring a continued approach in the implementation of the different DRRM programs in the City. Having a point of convergence it is important to ensure that risk reduction measures are being implemented in the city. This will help in reducing people and institutional vulnerabilities to disasters. This will likewise promote the paradigm shift towards mainstreaming DRRM in local planning by investing risk-reduction measures from the reactive mode exemplified by activities limited to disaster response operations.

Research, Technology Development and Knowledge Management

With the changes in the climate and technological advances, regularly conducting research and technology development will contribute to more innovative and adaptive mechanisms and approaches towards DRRM and CCA. Along side new information, knowledge management through database development, documentation, recognition and replication of good practices will help achieve the objectives and targets of the RDRRMP through more efficient use of resources, learnings and experiences.

Networking and partnership building between and among stakeholders, media and tiers of government

No one can claim to have monopoly of creating a safe, adaptive and disaster resilient communities. Henceforth, participation and coordination of other sectors in the City MUST always be the preeminent concern of the Davao City DRRM. Success in the implementation of the programs/projects is highly dependent on the close collaboration and cooperation of the different stakeholders. Building effective and mutually reinforcing partnerships and evolving networks ensure the multi-stakeholder and multi-sectorial participation of the different players in DRRM.

Pre-disaster Recovery Planning for Faster Post-disaster Rehabilitation and Recovery

Whenever a disaster occurs in the region, there is always great pressure to quickly reconstruct damaged infrastructures and critical other facilities. Pre-disaster recovery planning will help in a more effective Davao City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Plan Page 65

Monitoring and Evaluation

Feedback mechanisms are important aspects of gauging performance targets and learning from experiences on the ground. The Davao City DRMRP, being a term plan will definitely be anchored on the political term of an existing LGU administration and leadership, hence, review can be done after the term of has ended to attuned to the thrust of the sitting chairperson of the council.

--- [Page 75] ---
Monitoring and evaluation are essential components of results-based programming in DRRM as these will ensure that the plan's on-going implementation and that lessons from past experiences become input to the plan altogether. Also, through monitoring and evaluation activities, appropriate and needed revisions and/or changes can be identified, from which the plan can be revised and/or implemented again, in case even more appropriate ones are realized. These will be done by the Davao City DRRMC, in close coordination with the Davao City DRRMC's Four (4) lead agencies, and will focus on relevance, effectiveness, impact, and sustainability. A standard monitoring and evaluation template will be done by the City Planning and Development Office and the Davao City DRRMC in consultation with different stakeholders.

Monitoring and evaluation will use the indicators, targets and activities identified in each of the four thematic areas of the Davao City DRRMP. The indicators set will be applicable to both the City and barangays. Customization of the targets will depend on the risk assessments and analysis done in the different barangays and communities in the City. Different outputs and activities will be monitored by the different DRRMCs' chairperson to make sure that the activities are delivered efficiently and effectively.

This will also include an audit report on the use and utilization of the DRRMRF and how the said fund contributed to the implementation of the Plan.

Throughout all activities, ensuring "Faster, Adaptive and Disaster-Resilient Davao City Communities toward Sustainable Development" will be the main focus. It will be evident that the learning is captured and shared amongst the various stakeholders, leads and partners. Throughout its implementation, reporting on the progress on the Plan will be communicated through various media and partners, making sure that the learning are shared effectively. These will then feed into the Monitoring and Evaluation activities, it will be essential to link up the learning from the Davao City DRRMP implementation with that of other related plans.
