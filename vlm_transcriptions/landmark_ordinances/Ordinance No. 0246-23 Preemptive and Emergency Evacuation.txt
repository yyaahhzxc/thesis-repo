AN ORDINANCE ESTABLISHING THE INTEGRATED PREEMPTIVE AND EMERGENCY EVACUATION SYSTEM IN CASE OF EMERGENCIES CAUSED BY NATURAL OR MAN-MADE HAZARDS OR DISASTERS AND PROVIDING PENALTIES THEREFOR

Ord. No. 0246-23

Be it ordained by the Honorable Sangguniang Panlungsod of Davao City, in session
assembled, that:

SECTION 1. TITLE - This Ordinance shall be referred to as "THE PREEMPTIVE
AND EMERGENCY EVACUATION ORDINANCE OF DAVAO CITY".

SECTION 2. DECLARATION OF POLICY - Under Section 16, General Welfare
Clause of the Local Government Code, Local Government Units are authorized to exercise
the powers necessary, appropriate, or incidental to its efficient and effective governance
and those which are essential to the promotion of the general welfare.

Furthermore, under Section 2 of R.A. No. 10121, otherwise known as the Philippine
Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010, the State aims to recognize the
local identity of each province, city, and municipality of the Philippines for risk
reduction and management through decentralized powers, responsibilities, and resources
at the regional and local levels, and strengthen the capacities of LGUs and communities
in mitigating and preparing for, responding to, and recovering from the impact of
disasters.

Therefore, it is the declared policy of the City Government of Davao to protect the
lives and properties of its residents at all times, and, whenever possible, mitigate the
effects of any man-made and natural disasters within its locality.

SECTION 3. DEFINITION OF TERMS - As used in this Ordinance, the following
terms are hereby defined:

(a) Accredited Community Disaster Volunteers (ACDV) - are individuals or
organized volunteers who possess the requisite qualifications, as stipulated in
the guidelines currently utilized by CDRRMO, and whose names were submitted
as official Community Disaster Volunteers, Civil Risk Reduction and Management
Council (CDRRC), List of Community Disaster Volunteers, and/or the National Roster
of ACDVs maintained by the Office of Civil Defense (OCD) and have the following
roles:
i. Provide voluntary service to Local Government Units and communities for
DRRM-related activities.
ii. Ensure that services rendered are done in a professional manner and does
not compromise the safety of the volunteers and communities they are assisting.

(b) Community Volunteer Responders (CVR) - a group of responders in the
community that perform response activities.

(c) Disaster - a serious disruption of the functioning of a community or a society
involving widespread human, material, economic, or environmental losses and
impacts, which exceeds the ability of the affected community or society to cope
using its own resources. For purposes of this Ordinance, disaster shall include
both natural calamities and man-made disasters.

(d) Disaster Prevention - the outright avoidance of adverse impacts of hazards
and other related disasters.

(e) Disaster Victims - persons or group of persons who have been adversely affected by a natural or human-induced hazard who have to leave their habitual places of residence due to existing or impending threats, damaged shelter units, with casualty among immediate family members or those who remained in their habitual places of origin when still habitable but whose main source of income or livelihood had been damaged and are experiencing hopelessness and difficulty in coping or responding to the onslaught of the hazardous events on their own resources.

(f) Early Warning System (EWS) - refers to the set of capacities needed to generate and disseminate timely and meaningful warning information to enable individuals, communities, and organizations threatened by a hazard to prepare and act appropriately and insufficient time to reduce the possibility of harm or loss.

(g) Emergency - an unforeseen or sudden occurrence, especially danger, demanding an immediate action.

(h) Emergency Evacuation - means an evacuation ordered by competent authority when an unforeseen disaster has suddenly occurred which requires immediate evacuation of the affected area/s.

(i) Evacuation - means an operation whereby all or part of a particular population is temporarily relocated, whether individually or in an organized manner, from an area in which a disaster or emergency has been declared and which is considered dangerous to the health and safety of the general public.

(j) Evacuation Area, Evacuation Center, or Evacuation Place - a place where the Disaster Victims, Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), and Vulnerable and Marginalized Groups are brought before, during, and after calamities or disasters such as, but not limited to, typhoons, floods, landslides, earthquakes, fires, for care, safety, and protection.

(k) Hazard - a dangerous phenomenon, substance, human activity, or condition that may cause loss of life, injury or other health impacts, property damage, loss of livelihood and services, social and economic disruption, or environmental damage.

(l) Imminent Danger - means any condition in any place such that a danger exists which could reasonably be expected to cause death or serious physical harm immediately upon or before the occurrence of any human-induced or natural disasters.

(m) Indigenous Early Warning System (IEWS) - a community's traditional way of giving Early Warning, such as batingag and observing animal behavior, celestial bodies, and other natural phenomena or indicators of hazards. Whatever works for the barangay is acceptable as long as the meaning and the recommended action are properly disseminated to the community and constantly practiced through the conduct of drills and other skills enhancement exercises.

(n) Internally Displaced Person (IDP) or Persons Displaced by the Disaster (PDP) - refers to persons or groups of persons who have been forced or obliged to flee or to leave their homes or places of habitual residence, in particular as a result of or in order to avoid the effects of natural or human-induced disasters, and who have not crossed an internationally recognized State border.

(c) Preemptive Evacuation - means an evacuation ordered as a resort when a disaster has been declared and danger or loss of life is imminent, and conditions exist that may critically imperil or endanger the lives of those in a defined area.  

(p) Public Alert and Warning System (PAWS) - a system used to give warning to the public, such as a Siren, to alert the public of impending disaster occurrence. PAWS is usually science-based technology and mostly, those that are state-of-the-art devices that have wider coverage in terms of monitoring and alerting, which benefits responsiveness to emergency management and public safety officials, and builds public trust, equity and confidence.  

(q) Recognized Volunteer Groups (RVG) - are volunteer groups not included under the definition of ACDOs but are recognized by the City Government of Davao or LDRMC.  

(r) Reconstruction - the medium- and long-term rebuilding and sustainable restoration of resilient critical infrastructures, services, housing, facilities and livelihoods required for the full functioning of a community or a society affected by a disaster, aligning with the principles of sustainable development and "build back better," to avoid or reduce future disaster risk.  

(s) Recovery - the restoring or improving of livelihoods and health, as well as economic, physical, social, cultural and environmental assets, systems and activities, of a disaster-affected community or society, aligning with the principles of sustainable development and "build back better," to avoid or reduce future disaster risk.  

(t) Rehabilitation - the restoration of basic services and facilities for the functioning of a community or a society affected by a disaster.  

(u) Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) - a standard operating procedure is a set of step-by-step instructions compiled by an organization to help workers carry out routine operations. SOPs aim to achieve efficiency, quality output, and uniformity of performance while reducing miscommunication and failure to comply with the organization's regulations.  

(v) Vulnerable Groups - include individuals or groups of people that face higher exposure to disaster risk and poverty including, but not limited to, women, especially pregnant and lactating mothers, children and youth, especially orphans and unaccompanied children, elderly, differently-abled people, and people with chronic illnesses.

CDRMO and BDRRMC should conduct regular checking of the PAWS that are already established. The following are the PAWS employed by the City, but shall not be limited to the following:  

(a) Electronic Siren;  

(b) Manual Bell;

SECTION 5. CONSIDERATION IN THE IMPLEMENTATION OF PREEMPTIVE EVACUATION - The following protocols shall be observed in the implementation of preemptive evacuation:

(a) Upon the assessment and monitoring relative to imminent danger caused by weather disturbance and other hazards, the CDRRMO Operation Center through the Head of Office shall immediately inform the City Mayor and seek clearance for the implementation of evacuation procedures and protocols.

On the basis of such recommendation, the City Mayor shall immediately declare the implementation and enforcement of preemptive evacuation, which shall be guided by the following factors:

i. Conditions for Evacuation;  
ii. Chain of Command;  
iii. Evacuation Procedures;  
iv. Emergency Standards; and  
v. Early Warning System.

(B) The Preemptive Evacuation shall be enforced in three different levels, as follows:

i. Level 1 refers to a voluntary evacuation during Green Warning Signal;  
ii. Level 2 refers to the evacuation of vulnerable sectors, within four (4) hours, enforced during Yellow Warning Signal; and  
iii. Level 3 refers to complete evacuation, within four (4) hours, enforced during Orange and Red Warning Signal. Here, inhabitants or would-be disaster victims are mandated to evacuate in view of an imminent danger. However, if inhabitants or would-be disaster victims refuse or resist to vacate, he or she may be forcibly taken if the situation warrants as a means of ensuring his or her safety.

(c) If anyone within the affected area does not adhere to the order of evacuation during a Level 2 or Level 3 pre-emptive evacuation, the person may be compulsorily taken, if the situation warrants as a means of ensuring his or her safety;

SECTION 6. CONSIDERATION IN THE IMPLEMENTATION OF EMERGENCY EVACUATION - These protocols shall be observed in the implementation of emergency evacuations:

(a) Emergency Evacuation is a full evacuation of all residents and occupants within the hazard or disaster area and will be resorted to in cases where the forecasted impact of the hazard or disaster is within less than two (2) hours or in any incidents that are about to occur, are already occurring, or have already occurred, such as in the case of fire, earthquake, terrorist attack, or criminal activity within an area;

(b) Emergency Evacuation shall be carried out in a manner that is humane, respectful of the dignity of a person, not using disproportionate force, without discrimination, and with conscious attention to the needs of vulnerable sectors, prioritizing children, pregnant women, the elderly, Persons With Disabilities (PWDs), and those with chronic illnesses;

(c) Personnel and volunteers carrying out the emergency evacuation shall make sure that all measures are taken to inform the evacuees, in a manner and language that they can understand, of the need for a fast and orderly evacuation, and the lack of other alternatives for their evacuation.

(d) If anyone within the affected area does not adhere to the order of emergency evacuation or is found within the danger or target area beyond the above mentioned time frame, the person may be compulsorily taken, if the situation warrants, as a means of ensuring his or her safety;

SECTION 7. BARANGAY EVACUATION PLAN - It shall be incumbent upon the BDRRMCs to prepare an Evacuation Plan in their respective localities or areas of jurisdiction prior to a hazard or disaster and identify a Permanent Evacuation Center and possible Alternative/Temporary Evacuation Centers to be utilized in case of emergencies or disasters.

BDRRMCs shall post copies of the Evacuation Plan in conspicuous places within their respective localities or areas of jurisdiction and conduct annual drills.

All emergency measures adopted shall be a coordinated and collaborative effort between the City Government and the assisting agencies.

The Local Government Unit (LGU) shall have the authority to negotiate with owners of buildings/facilities with strong/safe structures to be made as temporary evacuation centers, prior to, during, and after the occurrence of disasters.

SECTION 8. ESTABLISHMENT OF CITY-WIDE EVACUATION SITES - In case of disasters or hazards, whether foreseen or unforeseen, which affect the entire City or a majority of its territory, there shall be designated evacuation sites within the City or in the vicinity thereof, which shall be notified to the LGUs and CDRRMCs with the majority of the city area is affected, in coordination with other LGUs, as determined by the CDRRMC. These areas will be confirmed by the City Government of Davao, through the appropriate act, agreement, order, resolution, or ordinance, as warranted by law.

SECTION 9. ESTABLISHMENT OF EVACUATION SITES AT THE COMMUNITY LEVEL. - There shall be established evacuation sites, both Permanent and Alternative, to be utilized by the affected area/s during hazards or disasters:

(a) Permanent Evacuation Sites: - There shall be an established permanent evacuation site per barangay as evaluated and designated by the CDRRMIC in coordination with the BDRRMC, to serve as shelter for people in times of calamities and emergencies. The BDRRMC shall conduct regular checking and inspection of the said evacuation sites.

(b) Alternative Evacuation Sites: - In anticipation of permanent evacuation sites, as designated under Section (a), being unable, totally, or partially, to accommodate Disaster Victims, IDPs, PDPs, and Vulnerable Groups due to fortuitous events or disasters, the BDRRMC shall designate alternative evacuation sites:

(i) Prior to the designation of alternative evacuation sites, the same shall first be evaluated and approved by the CDRRMO. Upon CDRRMO's approval, the BDRRMC shall release a resolution to officially designate the areas or locations where the alternative evacuation sites will be located. The BDRRMC shall conduct regular checking and inspection of the said alternative evacuation sites.

(ii) In case of private establishments and public schools, the BDRRMC shall have the authority to contract with the owners of said private establishments or principals of said public schools, as the case may be, in order to accommodate IDPs, and Vulnerable Groups. Provided, That a Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) shall be entered into by and between the BDRRMC and the owner of the private establishment or the principal of the public school, as the case may be.

(c) By order of preference, the following may be utilized as evacuation sites:

(1) Public gymnasiums, auditoriums and other open spaces situated within the Barangay;

(2) Public chapels situated within the Barangay;

(3) Public schools, child development centers or other places of worship within the Barangay;

(4) Private establishments within the Barangay;

(5) Permanent or Alternative Evacuation Sites of neighboring Barangays.

(d) In all evacuation sites, there must be an established option for transitional shelters, prioritizing vulnerable groups. New transitional shelters shall be designed with the following considerations: gender-specific emergency latrines, bathing cubicles, and hand washing facilities specifically designed for children. It shall provide mother and child-friendly spaces where children can take part in child activities. It shall also have provisions for maternal and newborn and infant care and rooms to protect, feed, provide personal care, and ensure the right to privacy. Existing transitional shelters shall be modified to the extent possible to comply with the foregoing considerations.

(e) The City Health Office (CHO) shall ensure that all disaster victims and IDPs are provided with proper and necessary medical and psychological assistance. CHO shall also enhance and implement policies and programs to be enforced during pre-emptive and emergency evacuation in order to guide and protect humanitarian aid workers, communities, health professionals, government aid agencies, and donors.

(f) The CSVDW shall create and enforce evacuation policies within the evacuation camp and its vicinity, with the assistance of the DCPD and other law enforcement agencies.

SECTION 10. RETURN, REHABILITATION, AND RECONSTRUCTION (RRR) - The CDRMO, together with the BDRRMC, and other concerned agencies, shall implement the following post-disaster/post-emergency guidelines:

(a) Return - Provided that the affected area is or has not been declared "No Build Zone," "High Risk Area," "Danger Zone," or "Hazard Prone Area," after the disaster or hazard has already subsided, that the affected areas are declared safe for habitation and for the IDPs to return to the said areas.

(i) The Incident Commander during a hazard or disaster shall monitor, assess, and report to the City Mayor on whether the affected area is considered "Safe," "No Build Zone," "High Risk Area," "Danger Zone," or "Hazard Prone Area."

(ii) In case the affected area is or has been declared a "No Build Zone," "High Risk Area," "Danger Zone," or "Hazard Prone Area," no person may return to the area without proper authorization from the Incident Commander or City Mayor, upon the recommendation of concerned agencies.

(iii) No person may continue to reside or occupy any portion of land which has been declared by the City Government of Davao, upon recommendation of concerned agencies, as a "No Build Zone," "High Risk Area," "Danger Zone," or "Hazard Prone Area." Any occupant in such an area may be removed by the DCPO, in coordination with the BDRRMC, and other concerned agencies.

(iv) No person, other than those authorized by the Incident Commander or the City Mayor, may return to the disaster or hazard area prior to the issuance of an official Notice to Return. The DCPO, in coordination with the BDRRMC, and other concerned agencies shall ensure that the affected area within the respective territorial jurisdiction of the barangay shall be cordoned off or shall be made inaccessible to persons other than those who are authorized by the Incident Commander or City Mayor. They shall also ensure that no unauthorized person enters the disaster or hazard area without the Notice to Return being issued.

(v) The BDRRMC, in coordination with the DCPO, and other law enforcement agencies, shall ensure the orderly return of the victims or IDPs to their residences within the affected areas only after the Notice to Return has been issued.

(b) Rehabilitation - The CPDQ and CEO, in coordination with other concerned agencies, shall strengthen their rehabilitation plans and processes and focus on pre-disaster activities to prevent or mitigate the occurrence of future disasters, whether natural or man-made, as well as post-disaster activities. The CDRMO, in coordination with the BDRRMC, shall assist the residents of areas affected by a natural or man-made disaster, in conducting rehabilitation activities; and

(c) Reconstruction - The CPDO and CEO, in coordination with other concerned agencies, shall promote the cooperation of diverse institutions, government agencies, and related stakeholders at all levels, including affected communities and businesses, in view of the complex and costly nature of post-disaster reconstruction. In this regard, the principle of "Build Back Better" shall be incorporated, where post-disaster reconstruction shall not only be restoration to its pre-disaster condition, but incorporate a higher level of resiliency standards, as well as climate change mitigation measures in all post-disaster recovery interventions.

SECTION 1.1. PERSONS IN AUTHORITY IN CASE OF EVACUATIONS - The following shall be authorized to initiate or assist a preemptive or emergency evacuation in a particular area: Provided, they properly identify themselves to the affected persons and explain the reason for the evacuation:

(a) City Officials;

(b) Barangay Officials;

(c) Law Enforcement Officers as Agents of Persons in Authority.

Persons in Authority in case of preemptive and emergency evacuations shall have the presumption of regularity in the performance of their official duties, and shall not be held responsible for any damage inflicted upon any person or property during the conduct of such duties, that aids to the effective and unhampered administration of government functions, as a state of necessity and in view of avoiding a greater evil or injury, unless such damage is inflicted in a willful or malicious manner.

SECTION 1.2. PROHIBITED ACTS - Any person or group of people who commits any of the following prohibited acts shall be held liable and be subjected to the penalties as prescribed in Section 13 of this Ordinance:

(a) Conducting relief operation without prior coordination with the operation center managed by the CSWDO, with the assistance of the Barangay Council, thereby causing havoc and commotion in the affected areas;

(b) Accessing or returning to the declared hazard site despite an outstanding order by competent authorities still being an off-limit area to any human activity, except government authorities doing assessment or evaluation and the like;

(c) Preventing the entry of government-owned equipment units, tools, and accessories, including key personnel to disaster-stricken areas by any individual;

(d) Dereliction of duties or refusal of key government employees to respond and act during times of calamities;

(e) Diverting the use of government equipment, tools, and resources to other uses than their intended usage;

(f) Maliciously damaging or destroying fences, barrier or any enclosure of the disaster affected areas which are purposely mounted for public security;

(g) Willfully and forcefully causing delay in the conduct of rescue operations, resisting or refusing to be evacuated despite imminent danger and or the declaration of pre-emptive or emergency evacuation;

(h) Tampering, damaging, or stealing hazard monitoring and early warning equipment and other related equipment and paraphernalia;

(i) Threatening, harassing, abusing, or harming any person in authority or its agents, camp managers, and other personnel in evacuation camps and its vicinity implementing the evacuation procedures;

SECTION 13. PENALTY CLAUSE - Any person, natural or juridical, if found to have not complied with any provisions of this Ordinance, shall be subjected to the following penalties:

(a) A fine of One Thousand Pesos (P1,000) for committing the act under subsection (a) of Section 12;

(b) A fine of Three Thousand Pesos (P3,000) for committing the acts under subsections (b), (c), (d), (e), and (f) of Section 12;

(c) A fine of Five Thousand Pesos (P5,000) and/or imprisonment not exceeding one (1) month at the discretion of the court for committing the acts under subsections (g), (h), (i), (j), (k), (l), (m) and (n) of Section 12.

In case of juridical persons, the President, the General Manager, or the individual entrusted with the administration thereof at the time of the commission of the violation shall be held responsible or liable thereto.

SECTION 14. NO-CONTEST PROVISION - Any person cited for violation of this Ordinance who does not wish to contest the same and is willing to pay voluntarily within thirty (30) days the fine imposed under this Ordinance prior to the filing of a complaint before the Office of the City Prosecutor shall be allowed to do so, and shall pay the corresponding administrative fine as cited in Section 11 at the Office of the City Treasurer. Availment of the no-contest provision shall exempt a person from criminal liability under this Ordinance.

The City Legal Office and the City Treasurer's Office shall provide for the procedure for the avallment of the "No-Contest" Provision and establish a case inventory and recording system of all violations of this Ordinance.

All proceeds from the payment of the herein imposed fine shall be appointed as follows:

SECTION 15. PLANNING, TRAINING AND ORIENTATION ON DISASTER RISK REDUCTION AND MANAGEMENT - The Council's approved training providers must plan and carry out trainings and orientations on disaster risk reduction and management in the community as well as planning, preparing, preventing, and mitigating against natural and man-made hazards, vulnerabilities, and climate change risk. As required by Republic Act No. 10121, planning, training, drills, and simulation exercises must be carried out at least once per quarter of the year.

SECTION 16. INFORMATION AND EDUCATION CAMPAIGN - There shall be a Public Information Drive regarding the implementation of this Ordinance, which shall be headed by the City Information Office (CIO) in coordination with concerned agencies. The CIO should likewise coordinate with the private sector to disseminate information on disaster preparedness. Information dissemination through signages, flyers, audio-visual presentations, and other forms of public information dissemination shall be established and placed. The concerned agencies shall coordinate with the CIO for the dissemination of information before, during, and after the occurrence of disasters.

SECTION 17. REVIEW OF PREEMPTIVE AND EMERGENCY EVACUATION ORDINANCE - The CDRRMO, BFP, and other concerned agencies shall review the guidelines provided in this Ordinance every three (3) years to ensure that the emergency responses are appropriate for natural and man-made disasters or hazards.

SECTION 18. REPEALING CLAUSE - Any ordinance, order, memorandum, rule and regulation which is inconsistent with the provisions of this Ordinance is hereby modified or repealed accordingly.

SECTION 19. SEPARABILITY CLAUSE - If, for any reason, any section of this Ordinance is declared unconstitutional or invalid, other sections or provisions hereof which are not affected thereby, shall continue to be in full force and effect.

SECTION 20. EFFECTIVITY - This Ordinance shall take effect fifteen (15) days after its publication in a newspaper of general circulation within the City.
