**Section 1. TITLE -** This Ordinance shall be known as "AN ORDINANCE FIXING THE SCHEDULE OF FAIR MARKET VALUES OF REAL PROPERTIES WITHIN THE TERRITORIAL JURISDICTION OF DAVAO CITY FOR THE 2008 GENERAL REVISION WHICH SHALL TAKE EFFECT IN THE YEAR 2009";

**Section 2. CRITERIA -** The criteria for the sub-classification of residential, commercial and industrial lands as well as memorial parks in the City of Davao shall be as follows:

**A. RESIDENTIAL LAND (URBAN)**

1. **First Class Residential Land:**
   a. Located along concrete roads.
   b. Where playground, school, light and telephone are installed.
   c. Located near public markets, malls, supermarkets in highly commercial areas.
   d. Religious institution and water supply are available in the said area.

2. **Second Class Residential Land:**
   a. Located along concrete roads.
   b. Where the land area is predominantly used as residential.
   c. Educational, religious institutions, water supply, market, church and playground are available.
   d. The condition of the residential houses are above average.

3. **Third Class Residential Land:**
   a. Located along mixed concrete and asphalt roads.
   b. Where average earning people reside, telephone, water, electricity and land transportation are available.
   c. Where the average highest value of land can be found.

4. **Fourth Class Residential Land:**
   a. Located along gravel and dusty roads.
   b. Where average and semi-strong material houses are constructed.
   c. Water supply, electricity, school, public market and church are far from the place and the way of living is below average.

5. **Low-cost housing by GSIS, SSS, Pag-ibig, etc.**

6. **Relocation Site by NHA, SIR, UDHA, BLISS, etc.**

7. **Residential lots in other barrios (interior lots).**

8. **Residential lots in the remote barangays.**

9. **Other far-flung residential rural lands.**

B. COMMERCIAL/INDUSTRIAL LANDS:

1. First Class:
a. Located along wide concrete roads.
b. Where trading centers and commercial activities of the city take place.
c. Where high grade commercial /industrial business establishments are located.
d. Traffic flow is exceptionally busy.
e. Where the highest land value is located.

2. Second Class:
a. Located along concrete roads, where trading centers and commercial activities are lower than the first class commercial land.
b. Where commercial/industrial establishments can be located, water supply, telephone and electricity are abundant.
c. The flow of vehicular transportation is slower than the first class.

3. Third Class:
a. Located along mixed concrete and asphalt roads, where trading centers and educational institutions are located; telephone services, electricity and water supply are just enough.
b. Land value is lower than the second class.

4. Fourth Class:
a. Located along asphalt and gravel roads.
b. Where trading activity is not so busy, electricity, water supply and telephone lines are in place, average grade commercial buildings and establishments are built.
c. Land value is lower than third class.

5. Fifth Class:
a. Located along gravel roads.
b. Where trading activity is not so busy, electricity, water system and telephone services are in place, average grade commercial/industrial buildings and establishments are built.
c. Land value lower than fourth class.

6. Sixth Class:
a. Located along earth roads.
b. Where trading activity is not so busy, electricity, water system and telephone services are in place, average grade commercial/industrial buildings and establishments are built.
c. Land value lower than fifth class.

7. Seventh Class:
a. Located along dirt roads.
b. Where trading activity is not so busy, electricity, water system and telephone services are in place, average grade commercial/industrial buildings and establishments are built.
c. Land value is lower than sixth class.

8. Eighth Class:
a. Located along improvised dusty roads.
b. Where trading activity is not so busy, electricity, water system and telephone services are in place, average grade commercial/industrial buildings and establishments are built.
c. Land value is lower than seventh class.

9. Ninth/other commercial/industrial rural lands

C. RURAL LANDS:
Lands found in the Districts of Talomo, Agdao, Buhangin, Toril, Calinan, Bunawan, Tugbok, Baguio, Marilao, and Paquibato are separately classified as Residential, Commercial, and Industrial lands also, which are lower than Residential, Commercial, and Industrial lands found in the Poblacion.

Section 5. GENERAL PROVISIONS:

The following general provisions shall be considered in determining the
schedule of base market value of residential, commercial and industrial lands
as well as memorial parks:

1. As far as practicable, this schedule of base market value shall be
controlling, but where the property to be assessed is a kind not
classified in this schedule or any kind for which a value is not
herein fixed, it shall be appraised at the current and fair market
value, independent of this schedule.

2. As a general rule, one hundred percent (100%) base value per
square meter for urban, Commercial, Industrial, and Residential lands shall be applied to all lands within the first forty (40)-meter
strip on commercial and residential sections and within fifty (50)
meters of industrial zones fronting asphalted or concrete streets
or roads. This foregoing rule shall not apply to residential and
commercial subdivisions.

3. Lands beyond the standard depth of twenty (20) meters for
residential land, thirty (30) meters for commercial land and fifty
(50) meters for industrial land shall be valued at eighty percent
(80%) for the second strip, sixty percent (60%) for the third strip and
forty (40%) of the base value for the remaining area. Provided,
however, that in case a parcel of land abuts two streets, or roads on
two sides with different base values, the stripping and valuation
thereof shall be based on the principal street or road with the higher
base value, but in no case shall the value of the last strip be lower
than the value provided for the other street.

4. For low and sunken areas of the land, a reduction from the base
value per square meter may be allowed due to the cost of filling and
compaction to bring the same at par with the adjoining developed lots. Provided, however, that such reduction will in no case exceed
thirty percent (30%) of the base value thereof.

5. Corner influence value of ten percent (10%) of the base value shall
be added to the valuation of lots situated at the corner of two streets
or roads. Provided however, that if the streets or roads have
different base values, the higher base value shall be used in the
computation thereof.

6. Vacant or idle land located in a purely residential area shall be
classified as residential. If such land is located in a purely
commercial area, the same shall be classified as commercial. If
located in a purely industrial area, the vacant or idle land shall be
classified as industrial.

7. Roads or streets in urban subdivisions, unless already donated and
turned over to the government shall be listed separately as taxable
in the name of the subdivision owner, and shall be valued uniformly
at seventy pesos ($70.00) per square meter if concrete, fifty pesos
($50.00) if asphalt. The assessment level to be applied must
conform with the classification of the area.

8. Parks and open spaces shall be valued at fifty percent (50%) of the base value applicable to the particular area where they are located and shall utilize the assessment level for the particular classification.

9. Private drainage, canals or creeks shall be valued at twenty percent (20%) of the base value applicable in the area and ten percent (10%) assessment level shall be applied.

10. Interior lots shall be valued at eighty percent (80%) of the base value of the lots fronting a street or road in that particular area. All other interior lots shall be valued in accordance with Rule 3 hereof.

11. All undeveloped parcels of land shall have seventy percent (70%) of the base value of the developed or improved lots located in that area.

12. Valuation of lands defined as Special Class under Par. D, Section 218 of Republic Act 7160, as amended, shall be based on the applicable unit value for residential, commercial or industrial lands as fixed in the schedule for the area where such land is located.

Section 8. GENERAL PROVISIONS ON THE APPRAISAL AND ASSESSMENT OF BUILDINGS AND OTHER STRUCTURES:

In order to ensure equitable levels of taxation, the following shall be observed in the appraisal and assessment of buildings and other structures:

1. The fair and current market values of all buildings or the schedule of unit base construction cost of buildings shall be used whichever is higher. However, for buildings and other structures constructed in low lying area or perennially flooded localities and in areas where adverse social and economic conditions prevail, a deduction of 15% to 20% may be subtracted from the unit base of construction cost on the type of buildings being appraised. This is to compensate for such difference in value of similar types of buildings constructed in ideal and highly developed environment.

2. This schedule of unit values shall be controlling but when the buildings or structures to be appraised are not covered by this schedule, it shall be appraised at its current and fair market value independent of this schedule and assessed for taxation purposes at the prescribed assessment level based on the actual use of the property.

3. The application of the minimum or maximum base value shall depend on the sound judgment of the appraiser or assessor based primarily on the quality of finish or workmanship of the building structures.

4. As a general rule, condominium buildings are assessed based on the separate values of the individual units within the condominium structures and are applied in accordance to assessment levels based on the market value of the respective condominium units.

5. The unit base values were derived from typical building design for each type of building, using aforementioned standard specifications. The use of the unit rates for "extra" or "deductions" shall be applied depending on the deviation of the construction of the building from the standard specifications, regardless of whether the minimum or maximum unit base value was used.

6. The schedule of market value for buildings shall be used for old buildings, structures and other improvements, but for new buildings, building permits, occupancy and completion, building plan and insurance shall be used whichever is higher as far as practicable.

Section 10. APPRAISAL OF MACHINERY:

10.1 The appraisal of machinery for tax purposes shall be based on its actual cost to the owner. To support the actual cost, the declared value or sworn statement from the owner or responsible officer, in case of a corporation, shall be secured to form as an integral part of the appraisal report.

10.2 For imported brand new machinery, the market value shall be based on its acquisition cost, which is the actual cost to the owner when the same is not yet depreciated or appraised within the year of its purchase, plus the cost of freight, insurance, bank and other charges, brokerage, arrastre and handling, duties and taxes, plus the cost of installation, installation, handling and installation charges at the present site. The cost in foreign currency shall be converted to its peso cost on the basis of the foreign exchange rates when such machinery was actually purchased as fixed by the Bangko Sentral ng Filipinas (BSP).

10.3 By the expressed provision of the Local Government Code, the acquisition cost of the machinery shall be based on the actual cost when it was acquired. The cost in foreign currency shall be converted to its peso cost based on the foreign exchange rate then prevailing when the same was acquired.

10.4 In all other cases, the cost in foreign currency of imported machinery shall be converted to its peso equivalent based on the exchange rates fixed by the BSP at the time of acquisition and apply a depreciation allowance of not exceeding 5% per year.

Section 11. GUIDELINES IN ESTIMATING DEPRECIATION BY THE
OBSERVED CONDITION METHOD:

Very Good Condition (80% - 100%) - This term describes an item or
equipment in excellent condition capable of being used to its fully
specified utilization and for its designed purpose without being modified
and not requiring any repairs or abnormal maintenance at the time of
inspection or within the foreseeable future.

Good Condition (55% - 75%) - This term describes those items or
equipment which have been modified or repaired and being used at or
near their fully specified utilization but the effects of age and/or
utilization in the foreseeable future.

Fair Condition (35% - 50%) - This term describes those items or
equipment which are being used at some point below their fully
specified utilization because of the effects of age and/or application
and which require general repairs and some replacement of minor elements
in the foreseeable future to raise level of utilization or near their original
specifications.

Poor Condition (15% - 30%) - This term describes those items of
equipment which can only be used at some point well below their fully
specified utilization and it is not possible to realize full capacity in their
current condition without extensive repairs and/or replacement of
major elements in the very near future.

Scrap Condition (0% - 10%) - This term is used to describe those
items of equipment which are no longer serviceable and which cannot
be utilized to any practical degree regardless of the extent of the repairs
or modifications to which they may be subjected (beyond economical
repair). This condition applies to items of equipment which have been
used for 100% technological obsolescent."

**Section 14. PENAL PROVISION:**

ART. 294 of R.A.7160 - Duty of Persons Acquiring Real Property or Making Improvements Thereon

(a) All persons, natural or juridical, or their duly authorized representatives, who acquire at any time a parcel or parcels of land in any city or municipality, shall file with the provincial, city, or municipal assessor of the locality where the property is located, a sworn statement declaring the true value thereof as appraised by the City Assessor after the acquisition of such property as evidenced by a duly notified or final Deed of Conveyance executed between the contracting parties bearing proof of registration from the Registrar of Deeds concerned. The sixty-day period shall commence on the date of execution of the Deed of Conveyance.

(b) In the case of houses, buildings or other improvements acquired or newly constructed which will require building permits, property owners or their authorized representatives, shall likewise file a sworn declaration of the true value of the subject house, building, or other improvement within sixty (60) days after:

(1) The date of a duly notified final Deed of Sale, contract, or other Deed of Conveyance covering the subject property executed between the contracting parties;

(2) The date of completion or occupancy of the newly constructed building, house, or improvement whichever comes earlier; and

(3) The date of completion or occupancy of any expansion, renovation, or additional structures or improvements made upon any existing building, house, or other real property, whichever comes earlier.

(c) In the case of machinery, the sixty-day period for filing the required sworn declaration of property values shall commence on the date of installation thereof as determined by the city or municipal assessor and, for this purpose, said local assessor may secure certification of the building official or engineer or other appropriate official stationed in the province, city, or municipality concerned.

(d) The sangguniang panlalawigan, sangguniang panlungsod, and sangguniang bayan shall enact their respective local ordinances providing for the imposition of penalties or pecuniary fines on property owners or administrators who fail to comply with this Article.

Failure of the owner, administrator or his authorized representative to declare real property within sixty (60) days from the date of sale, completion, occupancy, expansion and installation shall pay a fine of one fourth (1/4) of one percent (1%) of its market value or consideration but shall not be less than twenty-five Pesos [P25.00].
