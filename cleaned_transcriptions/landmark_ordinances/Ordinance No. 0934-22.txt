RESOLUTION NO. 03448-22
Series of 2022
ENACTING AN ORDINANCE DECLARING DAVAO CHONG HUA HIGH SCHOOL AS A HISTORICAL LANDMARK IN DAVAO CITY
WHEREAS, the Board of Trustees of Davao Chong Hua High School (formerly Davao Chinese High School) wrote to Mayor Sara Z. Duterte requesting to consider Davao Chong Hua High School as a historical landmark in Davao City;

--- [Page 3] ---
Page 2 of 2
Res. No. 03448-22

WHEREAS, the Davao Chinese High School was founded in 1924 and started classes in a rented space with two classrooms along San Pedro Street, with thirty (30) students and two (2) teachers;

WHEREAS, Don Francisco Juna Lim Villa Abrille (Lim Chuan Hun) donated a one-hectare lot situated along Sta. Ana Avenue for the school site then started the construction of the school building in October 1924 and was completed two months later. In January 1925, classes were held for the first time in the new school site;

WHEREAS, Davao Chong Hua High School has graduated thousands of Filipino-Chinese students through the years, teaching both Chinese and Filipino cultures and values, thus, in the truest sense "a cradle of heritage and culture... a living tradition";

WHEREAS, many of its graduates have become Davao civic leaders, medical practitioners, businessmen, philanthropists, engineers, teachers, and in all walks of life have made themselves useful and productive;

WHEREAS, the people of Davao City use the said school as a landmark because every time they go to school or even in establishments nearby the said school, they always tell the PUP driver "Chinese school lang ko Nong ha";

NOW THEREFORE, on motion of Councilor Pilar C. Braga, duly seconded by Councilors Conrad C. Balaran, Augusto Javier G. Camps III, and Bal Hundra Cassandra Dominique N. Advincula, be it resolved, as it is hereby resolved, TO ENACT AN ORDINANCE DECLARING DAVAO CHONG HUA HIGH SCHOOL AS A HISTORICAL LANDMARK IN DAVAO CITY;

RESOLVED FURTHER, that copies of this Resolution be furnished the City Mayor through the City Administrator's Office, the Vice-Mayor's Office, Museo Dabawenyo, the National Historical Commission of the Philippines, the Davao Historical Society, the Department of Education-Region XI, Davao Chong Hua High School and all other concerned offices/departments, for their information, guidance and appropriate action;

ADOPTED, April 19, 2022, by a unanimous vote of all the Members of the Sanggunian present, there being a quorum.

BE IT ORDAINED BY THE SANGUNJUNANG PANTULINGSOED OF DAVAO CITY IN SESSION ASSEMBLED, THAT:

SECTION 1. TITLE - This Ordinance shall be known as "AN ORDINANCE DECLARING DAVAO CHORCH HUA HIGH SCHOOL AS A HISTORICAL LANDMARK OF DAVAO CITY".

SECTION 2. DECLARATION OF PRINCIPLES. Article II, Section 3, (a) of Republic Act No. 10066 or the National Cultural Heritage Act of 2009 and II (B) (C) Guidelines on the Identification, Classification, and Recognition of Historic Sites and Structures of the Philippines under the National Historical Commission of the Philippines, provides respectively, to wit:

"(A) "Historical Landmarks" shall refer to structures that are associated with events or achievements significant to Philippine history as declared by the National Historical Commission of the Philippines;

(B) Properties that bear strong foreign historical or period influences such as Chinese, Arabic, Spanish, Mexican, American, or Japanese and those that provide strong evidence of historical relations with other countries;

(C) Sites of first establishments in the Philippines (e.g., site of the first printing press, commercial house, theater, school, transport system, distillery, etc.");

SECTION 3. THE FOUNDING PERIOD. In 1921, more than a thousand Chinese citizens lived in Davao. They formally opened the first Chinese school known as Davao Chinese High School on June 3, 1924 in a rented space along San Pedro Street. In October of the same year, Don Francisco Junta Lim Villa Abril donated the one-hectare lot at the corner of Sta. Ana Avenue and J.P. Laurel Avenue and started to construct a school building. After three months, thirty (30) students with the first Principal Mr. Lee Long Teo and two (2) teachers, Mr. Leong Ortega and Te Lum Tel, transferred to the new school site and started classes on January 1925.

SECTION 4. RECOGNITION AND INTEGRATION TO PHILIPPINE EDUCATIONAL SYSTEM. In October 1930, the Davao Chinese High School was given recognition by the Chinese Department of Education and Overseas Chinese Affairs Commission. It was not until August 9, 1938, that the school was accorded recognition by the Philippine Government. On April 23, 1937, under Presidential Decree No. 176, which merged all foreign schools, the institution was renamed Davao Central High School then, decades later, as Davao Chong Hua High School.

SECTION 5. THE WAR. On December 8, 1941, ten hours after the attack on Pearl Harbor, the Japanese Military invaded the Philippines. The war cost the lives of three board members of the school. The school then was turned into a Japanese boarding school. Only the roof of the school building was left. Still, only months after the liberation of the City, the school reopened in November 1945 with 345 students.

--- [Page 12] ---
**SECTION 6. HISTORICAL EVENT**

Davao Chong Hua High School is the first Chinese school in Davao City run by a foreign national. The site of the school was popularly known as "Dabawenyo" as "Chinese School" despite the fact that the said school was renamed "Davao." The school produced thousands of professionals who became part and parcel of the growth and development of Davao City through the teaching of both Chinese and Filipino cultures. The third largest Chinatown known as the Arch of Peace was built right beside the Davao Chong Hua High School. The school is located at corner 9ta. Amo Avenue and J.P. Laurel Avenue which part of the largest Chinatown in the Philippines with the total land area of 44 hectares and the only one in Mindanao.

**SECTION 7. CENTENNIAL YEAR**

Davao Chong Hua High School is the first private school to mark its 100 years of existence having been founded in 1924, which qualifies the same to be declared a historical landmark in Davao City.

**SECTION 8. SEPARABILITY CLAUSE**

If, for any reason, any section or provision of this Ordinance is declared unconstitutional or invalid, such sections or provisions shall not be affected by such declaration and shall continue to be in full force and effect.

**SECTION 9. EFFECTIVITY**

This Ordinance shall take effect immediately upon approval.

AN ORDINANCE DECLARING THE DAVAO CHONG HUA HIGH SCHOOL AS HISTORICAL LANDMARK OF DAVAO CITY
Be it ordained by the Honorable Sangguniang Panlungsod of Davao City in session assembled:
SECTION 1. Title. This Ordinance shall be known as "AN ORDINANCE DECLARING THE DAVAO CHONG HUA HIGH SCHOOL AS HISTORICAL LANDMARK OF DAVAO CITY".
SECTION 2. Declaration of Principles. Article II, Section 3, (s) of the National Cultural Heritage Act of 2009 (R.A. No. 10066) and II (B) (C) Guidelines on the Identification, Classification, and Recognition of Historic Sites and Structures of the Philippines under National Historical Commission of the Philippines, provides respectively, to wit:
"Historical landmarks" shall refer to sites or structures that are associated with events or achievements significant to Philippine history as declared by the National Historical Commission of the Philippines;
II Identification of Historic Sites and Structures
(B) Properties that bear strong foreign historical or period influences such as Chinese, Arabic, Spanish, Mexican, American, or Japanese and those that provide strong evidence of historical relations with other countries;
(C) Sites of first establishments in the Philippines (e.g., site of the first printing press, commercial house, theater, school, transport system, distillery, etc.);
SECTION 3. The Founding Period. In 1921, more than a thousand of Chinese citizens lived in Davao. They formally opened the first Chinese school known as the Davao Chinese High School in June 3, 1924 in a rented space in San Pedro Street. In October of the same year, Don Francisco Juna Lim Villa Abrilhe donated the one-hectare lot at the corner of Sta. Ana Avenue and J. P. Laurel Avenue and started to construct a school building. After three months, the thirty (30) students with the first Principal Mr. Lee Leng Tee and two (2) teachers: Ho Long Ortega and Te Lun Tek, transferred to the new school site and started classes on January 1925;

--- [Page 20] ---
**SECTION 4. Recognition and Integration to Philippine Educational System.** In October 1930, the Davao Chinese High School was given recognition by the Chinese Department of Education and Overseas Chinese Affairs Commission. It was not until August 9, 1938, that the school was accorded recognition by the Philippine government. On April 23, 1973, under Presidential Decree 176, which Filipinized all foreign schools, the institution was renamed Davao Central High School and, decades later, as Davao Chong Hua High School;

**SECTION 5. The War.** On December 8, 1941, ten hours after the attack on Pearl Harbor, the Japanese Military invaded the Philippines. The war cost the lives of three board members of the school. The school then was turned into a Japanese hospital. After the war, only one school building was left. Still, only months after the liberation of the city, the school reopened in November 1945 with 345 students;

**SECTION 6. Historical Event.** The Davao Chong Hua High School is the first Chinese school in Davao City run by a foreign national. The site of the school was popularly known by the Dabawenyo as "Chinese School", despite the fact that the said school was renamed twice. The school produced thousands of professionals who became part and parcel of the growth and development of Davao City through the teaching of both Chinese and Filipino cultures. The third Arch of Peace for Chinatown in Davao City were built right beside the Davao Chong Hua High School. The school is located at corner Sta. Ana Avenue and J.P. Laurel Avenue as part of the largest Chinatown in the Philippines with the total land area of 44 hectares in Davao City and the only one in Mindanao;

**SECTION 7. Centennial Year.** The Davao Chong Hua High School is the first private school to mark its 100 years of existence having been founded in 1924, which qualifies to a historical landmark in Davao City;

**BACKGROUND**

The City Mayor's Office had forwarded to the Committee on Education, Science & Technology, Arts and Culture the letter from the Board of Trustees of Davao Chong Hua High School (formerly the Davao Central High School) headed by its Chairman, Mr. Domingo T. Uy, requesting the Hon. Mayor Sara Duterte to consider Davao Chong Hua High School as one of the historical landmarks of Davao City.

Upon receipt of the said letters from the Office of the City Mayor, Councilor Pilar C. Braga then sponsored a resolution and passed under Suspended Rules during the Regular Session last September 7, 2021 a "Resolution Congratulating Davao Chong Hua High School for its Centennial Year" this coming 2024 and the proposed resolution declaring the said school as Historical Landmark were included in the First Reading and docketed as Item No. 1496 referred to the Committee on Education, Science and Technology, Arts and Culture.

They are appreciative on the passage of Ordinance No. 0330-20, Series of 2020 entitled "An Ordinance Requiring all Public and Private Schools in all Levels in Davao City, to include in their Curriculum the Teaching of the History of the City of Davao", for the students to value the rich heritage of our beloved City.

**HIGHLIGHTS OF THE COMMITTEE HEARING**

The Committee Hearing was conducted last November 18, 2021 at 1:00 in the afternoon via videoconference (via Zoom). The Chairperson acknowledged the presence of Mr. Amado So, one of the Board of Trustees of the Davao Chong Hua High School and Chairman of the Steering Committee Centennial Souvenir Program, of which he explained that Davao Chong Hua High School (DCHHS) founded in 1924 making it the oldest private school in Davao City... having contributed to the growth and development of Davao. Mr. Edgar Te, member of the Steering Committee, also stated that a lot of things happened within 100

--- [Page 29] ---
3

years in which they have produced many professionals like Engineers, Doctors and a Scientist in NASA.

They wanted their school to be part of the rich history of our City, since it was founded in 1924 when they formally opened in a rented space with two classrooms along San Pedro Street with 30 students and 2 teachers.

Davao Chinese School was formally opened in San Pedro Street on June 03, 1924.

At that time, Don Francisco Juna Lim Villa Abrille (Lim Chuan Hun) donated a one-hectare lot situated along Sta. Ana Avenue for the school site then started the construction of school building in October 1924 and was completed two months later. In January 1925, classes were held for the first time in the new school site.

--- [Page 30] ---
The Chairperson acknowledged the presence of Mr. Melchor C. Te, School Director of DCHHS, to present to the Committee the history of the Davao Chong Hua High School formerly known as Davao Central High School. He stated that their school was founded in 1924 and now almost 100 years in existence. He went on to enumerate the different growth periods:

1. Founding Period (1923-1926) Don Francisco Juna Lim Villa Abrille donated the one-hectare lot at corner Sta. Ana Avenue and J.P. Laurel Avenue;

2. Expansion Period (1926-1940) the school had first four principals, increasing of teaching staff from four to nine and increasing of students from 12 to 380;

3. Destruction and Revival Period (1940-1960) Battle of Davao;

--- [Page 31] ---
4. Transformation Period (1964-1983) National Government issued a Presidential Decree No. 176, Series of 1973, governing all foreign educational institution, other than those which are already established or which may hereafter be established by religious orders, mission boards and charitable organizations, shall be owned solely by citizens of the Philippines or corporations or associations at least sixty per centum (60%) of the capital of which is owned by such citizens and on 1966, Mr. Wee Siu Yen, Chairman of BOT donated a basketball court. Music Room was constructed in High School Department.

5. Reconstruction Period (1983-2004) Mr. Gilbert Go as the longest serving Chairman of the Board of Trustees for fifteen years. The school celebrated its Diamond Jubilee and inaugurated several school buildings and other school activities such as 60th Foundation and Teachers Day, first Parent-Teacher Association induction, and inauguration of Alumni Association Manila Chapter;

6. Challenging Period (2004-present) changing the name of school from Davao Central High School to Davao Chong Hua High School and also known as "Chinese School". Adopting the online teaching due to COVID-19 pandemic and they planning to modernize their school by installing air-conditioning in all classrooms and upgrading the preschool facilities.

Mr. Jacob Chua, Board of Trustees of DCHHS mentioned that the people use the aforesaid school as a landmark because every time they go to school, they always tell the PUJ driver "Chinese school lang ko nong ha".

Mr. Amado So pointed out that the Acacia Tree which is located inside the Davao Chong Hua High School is still there for many years now and they also requesting the City to declare the same as a Heritage Tree.

Forester Allan Matanog, NGP coordinator of the Community Environment and Natural Resources Office-DENR XI, said that the old Acacia Tree (Samanea saman) planted before the establishment of Davao Chong Hua High School formerly Davao Chinese High School in October 1924. It is propagated through inarching method with a diameter of 77cm (left side) and 140cm (right side). A silvicultural treatment was applied to control the crown of the tree to prevent the damage to the school building. Hence, they recommend and issued a certification as one of the requirements of National Historical Commission of the Philippines (NHCIP) to declare the old Acacia Tree as Monumental Tree and recognized as Historical Landmark of Davao City. Forester Matanog added that two stems joined together which is very unique among Acacia Trees.

--- [Page 33] ---
The Chairperson thanked the good gentlemen from CENRO-DENR XI and Mr. So for informing the Committee about the existence of the Acacia Tree inside the premises of Davao Chong Hua High School, but she suggested to declare it separately since the City Ordinance for Heritage Trees was recently approved by the 19th City Council.

After hearing all the manifestations of the stakeholders of the Davao Chong Hua High School, the Chairperson said that the Committee will assess if the said school have met all the criteria for declaring a Historical Landmark. The Chairperson then assured the proponent that she will favorably endorse for approval if everything is in proper order.

RECOMMENDATION:

After checking all the necessary requirements set forth by the National Historical Commission of the Philippines (NHCIP) for the Identification, Classification and Recognition of Historic Sites and Structures, the Committee on Education, Science & Technology, Arts and Culture, hereby recommends to enact an Ordinance Declaring the Davao Chong Hua High School as Historical Landmark of Davao City, in time for its 100 years anniversary or Centennial Years in 2024.

A Charter Member of the Davao Central Educational Association.

Don Francisco Villa Abrille Lim greatly valued the interest of education as exemplified by his donation of 1600 square meters of precious land to the DAVAO CENTRAL EDUCATIONAL ASSOCIATION for the campus site in October of 1924.
