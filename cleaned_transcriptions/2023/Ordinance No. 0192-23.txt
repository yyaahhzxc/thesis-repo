THE ANTI-BULLYING ORDINANCE OF DAVAO CITY

--- [Page 6] ---

Ord. No. 0192-23

Be it ordained by the Sangguniang Panlungsod of Davao City, in session assembled, that:

SECTION 1. TITLE - This Ordinance shall be known as "THE ANTI-BULLYING ORDINANCE OF DAVAO CITY."

SECTION 2. POLICY DECLARATION - It is hereby declared the policy of the City of Davao to ensure that the fundamental rights of persons to life, liberty, property, and security are promoted and protected. As a society composed of persons, the City recognizes the importance of individuality and the free exercise thereof as an essential ingredient of a well-functioning democracy. The practice of bullying, however, shall be met with disdain as it is a form of abuse which serves to deprive others of the free exercise of their own rights. In consonance with the national government's policy under Republic Act No. 10627 declaring acts of bullying as unrepeatable, Republic Act No. 11313 whereby all forms of bullying shall be prohibited, including but not limited to public spaces, online, workplaces, and educational institutions, all forms of bullying shall not be countenanced.

SECTION 3. ACTS OF BULLYING - "Bullying" as adopted from Republic Act No. 10627, shall refer to any severe or repeated act by one or more persons through a written, verbal, or electronic expression, or a physical act or gesture, or any combination thereof, directed at another individual that has the effect of actually causing or placing the latter in reasonable fear of physical or emotional harm to himself or damage to his property; creating a hostile environment at the school, workplace, or any other situation for the individual; infringing upon the rights of the individual; or materially and substantially disrupting the education process or the orderly operation of a school, workplace, or any other area, venue, or situation; such as, but not limited to, the following:

a) Any unwanted physical contact between the bully and the complainant, such as punching, pushing, shoving, kicking, slapping, tickling, headlocks, inflicting pranks, teasing, fighting and the use of available objects as weapons;

b) Any act that causes damage to an individual's psyche and/or emotional well-being;

c) Any slanderous statement or accusation that causes an individual undue emotional distress, such as directing foul language or profanity at the target, name-calling, tormenting and commenting negatively on an individual's looks, clothes and body;

d) Cyberbullying or any bullying done through the use of technology or any electronic means.

Bullying shall be further classified into four (4) aspects, namely:

a. Bullying in educational institutions

b. Bullying in the workplace

c. Cyberbullying

d. Bullying in other public places

Any person who commits the acts described above is considered a bully. Persons who, through the acts described above, retaliate against another person who reports acts of bullying or who provides information during an investigation or is a witness under such investigation, shall also be considered to have committed bullying.

--- [Page 7] ---
**SECTION 4. ACTS OF BULLYING IN EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS:**

To any severe, or repeated use by one or more students of a written, verbal, or electronic expression, or a physical act or gesture, or any combination thereof, directed at another student that has the effect of actually causing or placing the latter in reasonable fear of physical or emotional harm or damage to his property; creating a hostile environment at school for the other student; infringing on the rights of another student at school; or materially and substantially disrupting the education process or the orderly operation of a school, such as, but not limited to, the following:

1. Any unwanted physical contact between the bully and the victim like punching, pushing, showing, kicking, slapping, tickling, headlocks, inflicting school pranks, teasing, fighting, and the use of available objects as weapons;
2. Any act that causes damage to a victim's psyche and/or emotional well-being;
3. Any slanderous statement or accusation that causes the victim undue emotional distress like directing foul language or profanity at the target, name-calling, tormenting and commenting negatively on victim's looks, clothes and body.

Educational institutions covered under this Ordinance include public or private schools, regardless of educational level, such as:

a) Preparatory schools;
b) Primary schools;
c) Secondary schools;
d) Colleges and universities, including post-graduate;
e) Vocational schools;
f) Review centers, tutorial centers, and other similar establishments;
g) Training institutions by virtue of education such as On-the-Job training as well as other activities performed off-school, but for educational purpose/s.

Persons liable for bullying under this Section include, but are not limited to, the following:

i. Students;
ii. Trainees, interns, exchange students, and volunteers;
iii. Teachers, student-teachers and non-teaching staff;
iv. School Administrators;
v. Parents and guardians of students;
vi. Other authorized persons within the school campus;
vii. Visitors.

All acts and reports of bullying in the aforementioned institutions, and all acts of bullying by, among, and between the aforementioned persons shall be under the primary jurisdiction of the educational institution concerned.

**SECTION 5. ACTS OF BULLYING IN THE WORKPLACE:**

Acts of bullying in the workplace can be defined as the intentional and repeated less favorable treatment of a person by another or others in the workplace, which may be considered unreasonable and inappropriate workplace practice, but excludes acts that necessarily fall under management prerogative and discipline done in good faith and without violating employee rights. Management prerogative here refers to the employer's inherent right to regulate, according to his own discretion and judgment, all aspects of employment, including hiring, firing, transfer of employees, lay-off of workers, discipline, dismissal, and recall of employees.

Workplace bullying includes, but is not limited to, behavior that intimidates,
offends, degrades, or humiliates a worker, possibly in front of co-workers, clients,
customers, vendors, visitors, and other persons in a workplace setting or outside thereof,
by reason of such employment.

Acts of bullying in the workplace include the acts previously enumerated under
and other acts such as, but not limited to:

a. Frequently intimidating or undermining employees by demeaning their work
standards, not giving them credit, setting them up for failure, and reminding
them of old mistakes;

b. Uninterrupted threatening of employees' personal self-esteem and work
status;

c. Continuous isolation of employees from opportunities, information, and
interaction with others;

d. Repeatedly giving impossible deadlines, creating undue pressure and stress,
and overworking employees;

e. Giving constant and unfair criticism;

f. Persistent blaming without factual justification;

g. Frequently making unreasonable demands;

h. Giving hostile glares and other intimidating gestures repeatedly;

i. Constant yelling, screaming, and swearing;

j. Purposefully excluding or isolating a coworker;

k. Frequently making threats, threats, or notes;

l. Incessant monopolization of supplies and other resources;

m. Persistently engaging in excessive social bantering, teasing, and humiliation;

n. Deliberately insulting others and taking part in behind-the-back put-downs repeatedly;

o. Monitoring another excessively in a continuous manner;

p. Repeatedly stealing credit for other employees' work, except when the nature
of the work is collective or commissioned.

SECTION 6. CYBERIBELI - Cyberibel is the unlawful or prohibited act of libel
as defined in Article 355 of the Revised Penal Code, as amended, committed through
a computer system or any other similar means which may be devised in the future.

Computer systems refers to any device or group of interconnected or related
devices, one or more of which, pursuant to a program, performs automated processing
of data. It covers any type of device with data processing capabilities including,
but not limited to, computers, mobile phones. The device consisting of hardware and software
may include input, output, and storage components which may stand alone or be
connected in a network or other similar devices. It also includes computer data storage
devices or media.

SECTION 7. ADDRESSING BULLYING IN EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS
All educational institutions are hereby directed to adopt policies to address the existence
of bullying. Such policies shall be regularly updated and, at a minimum, shall include
provisions which:

A. Identify the range of disciplinary administrative actions that may be taken
against a respondent for bullying or retaliation which shall be commensurate
with the nature and gravity of the offense. Provided, that, in addition to the
disciplinary sanctions imposed upon a respondent of bullying or retaliation,
he/she shall also be required to undergo a rehabilitation program which shall
be administered by the institution concerned. The parents of the said
respondent shall be encouraged by the said institution to join the rehabilitation
program.

B. Establish clear procedures and strategies for:

1. Reporting acts of bullying or retaliation;
2. Responding promptly to and investigating reports of bullying or retaliation;
3. Restoring a sense of safety for a victim and assessing the student's need for protection;
4. Protecting from bullying or retaliation of a person who reports acts of bullying, provides information during an investigation of bullying, or is a witness to or has reliable information about an act of bullying; and
5. Providing counseling or referral to appropriate services for respondents, complainants, and appropriate family members of said students.

C. Enable students to anonymously report bullying or retaliation: Provided, however, that no disciplinary administrative action shall be taken against a respondent solely on the basis of an anonymous report;

D. Subject a student who knowingly makes a false accusation of bullying to disciplinary administrative action;

E. Educate students on the dynamics of bullying, the anti-bullying policies of the school as well as the mechanisms of such school for the anonymous reporting of acts of bullying or retaliation;

F. Educate parents and guardians about the dynamics of bullying, the anti-bullying policies of the school and how parents and guardians can provide support and reinforce such policies at home; and

G. Maintain a public record of relevant information and statistics on acts of bullying or retaliation in school: Provided, that the names of students who committed acts of bullying or retaliation shall be strictly confidential and only made available to the school administration, teachers directly responsible for the said students and parents or guardians of students who are on have been victims of acts of bullying or retaliation.

All educational institutions shall provide students and their parents or guardians a copy of the anti-bullying policies being adopted by the school. Such policies shall likewise be included in the school's student and/or parent handbook and shall be conspicuously posted on the school's walls and website. There shall also be training programs provided to teachers, administrators, and other employees to develop their knowledge and skills in preventing or responding to acts of bullying shall also be performed in these educational institutions.

SECTION 9. ADDRESSING BULLYING IN THE WORKPLACE - Workplaces are mandated to adopt anti-bullying policies necessary in line with the demands of their industry. However, all public and private offices and establishments in the City are directed to also adopt the mandatory policies and guidelines issued by the Civil Service Commission (CSC), City Human Resources Management Office, and Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) in order to address bullying and other similar acts in the workplace.

Workplaces categorized under the informal sector and those which do not require registration with DOLE are not covered under this Section and acts of bullying complained shall instead be considered as bullying in other public places as provided covered in the next succeeding section.

**SECTION 9. BULLYING IN OTHER PUBLIC PLACES**
This type of bullying covers those acts generally done outside of the educational institution or workplace, and also covers those acts done in a workplace as categorized under Section 8, paragraph 2.

**SECTION 10. ADDRESSING BULLYING IN OTHER PUBLIC PLACES**
In order to address bullying in other public places, Anti-Bullying Desks (Desk) shall be created in all Barangays and/or District Centers in the City. The existing Women's and Children Protection Desk Officers, Violence Against Women (VAH) Desk Officers, or their equivalent in the barangays shall also be empowered to act as Anti-Bullying Desk Officers. They shall be responsible for the enforcement of anti-bullying policies at the Barangay level.

Any person may report acts of bullying before the Desk in the Barangay where the offense was committed. The Desk shall have the authority to take a preliminary inquiry into the reported case. Should the Desk determine that the act complained of is either in an educational institution or a workplace, it shall be forwarded to the proper anti-bullying mechanism of the workplace or educational institution concerned.

The Desk shall refer all properly identified cases of bullying under this Section to the Barangay Justice System of the barangay where the offense was committed. Every report of bullying and any proceeding which concerns a minor shall at all times be represented and assisted by the legal guardian or next of kin, in the presence of a social worker or equivalent person.

Every person who admits to or is found to have committed bullying under the Barangay Justice System shall be also required to undergo a period of counseling and rehabilitation.

**SECTION 11. RESOLUTION OF BULLYING CASES**
Bullying cases shall be deemed resolved when:

a. It has been determined with finality by the appropriate body that no act of bullying took place;

b. There has been a settlement between the parties and the conditions of the same have been complied with; and

c. There has been a determination that bullying has been committed, and the respondent(s), the complainant(s), and their immediate families have undergone the requisite disciplinary measures and rehabilitation/intervention procedures, as may be provided in the concerned institution's internal mechanisms.

When a case is deemed resolved, no liability under this Ordinance shall ensue. Non-resolution of cases may be considered a ground for liability under Section 19 of this Ordinance.

**SECTION 12. CREATION OF A CITY ANTI-BULLYING COUNCIL**
The City shall establish the City Anti-Bullying Council (CABC), which shall be responsible for the establishment of policies related to bullying. These policies shall include, among others, the formulation of necessary education, prevention, counseling, and intervention measures. The CABC may, in the performance of its functions, request the listing of bullying complaints from educational institutions, workplaces, and barangays, in order to effect the purpose of this Ordinance in accordance with national laws against bullying. The CABC shall convene regularly at least once a month.

SECTION 13. CREATION OF CITY ANTI-BULLYING DESK - The City Anti-Bullying Desk (Desk) shall be created within any appropriate department of the City Social Welfare and Development Office (CSWDO), and shall be responsible for the effective implementation of this Ordinance.

The Desk shall oversee the other Anti-Bullying Desks in Barangays and District Centers. The Desk shall also function as the secretariat of CABC, and shall handle the day-to-day affairs of the latter.

The Desk is tasked to receive complaints of bullying in the city, and is empowered to forward cases to the proper forum for prompt disposition.

SECTION 14. CREATION OF AN ANTI-BULLYING HOTLINE - An anti-bullying telephonic hotline shall be created, which shall entertain calls for reporting of instances of bullying, requests for assistance or rehabilitation, psychosocial support, and other bullying-related concerns. This hotline shall be free of charge and shall maintain the privacy and confidentiality of its callers. In addition to this telephone hotline, online chat or email accessibility shall also be established, observing the same level of privacy and confidentiality as its telephone counterpart.

SECTION 15. REHABILITATION, INTERVENTION AND DIVERSION AND COUNSELING - An Intervention and Diversion program shall be designed for minor offenders who are held liable by the courts under this Ordinance. As for adults found liable, the Mental Health Division of the City Health Office (CHO) shall design and implement an appropriate counseling program.

SECTION 16. BULLYING PREVENTION MONTH - The month of October shall hereby be declared as the Davao City Bullying Prevention Month. During the said month, the City shall organize events and activities related to its anti-bullying policy. During this month, entities that are found compliant with the requisites for addressing bullying in their respective institutions shall be duly recognized and declared a Bullying-Free Establishment

**SECTION 17. ADMINISTRATIVE LIABILITY FOR INACTION OR NON-RESOLUTION OF CASES**

Educational institutions and/or workplaces which fail to take action on reports of bullying or fail to administratively resolve cases of bullying within their respective jurisdictions as required may be held liable as follows:

a. **First Offense:**
The educational institution and/or workplace concerned shall be invited to appear before the CABC to explain the reason for the non-resolution within the required period. Should the explanation be found unjustified, indolent, or unscrupulous, the educational institution or workplace shall be cited for violation of this Ordinance and shall be subject to an administrative fine of Five Thousand Pesos (PhP5,000.00).

b. **Second Offense and Subsequent Offenses:**
Subsequent offenses by any educational institution and/or workplace shall warrant the payment of an administrative fine of Five Thousand Pesos (PhP5,000.00). The CABC may also recommend the denial of those duties required by law, or the application for renewal of the business permit issued by the Office of the City Mayor.

The CABC shall further recommend to the proper authorities the filing of a complaint against the educational institution and/or workplace concerned, in accordance with pertinent substantive and procedural rules and relevant issuances of the Department of Education, Commission on Higher Education (CHED), the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE), Civil Service Commission (CSC), Department of Interior and Local Government (DILG), or any government agency or instrumentality having jurisdiction over the entity being complained of, as may be applicable.

**SECTION 18. PENALTIES**

Persons at the age of majority who have been administratively found to have committed acts of bullying or entered into settlement agreements in their respective forums but willfully refuse or unjustifiably fail to comply with the conditions and penalties imposed upon him/her shall be punished by imprisonment from three (3) to six (6) months and/or a fine ranging from Three Thousand Pesos (PhP3,000.00) to Five Thousand Pesos (PhP5,000.00), at the discretion of the court.

The aforementioned persons, whether a minor or an adult, shall also be required to enroll themselves into an appropriate intervention program which includes the following:

a. Counseling for the perpetrator(s)/bully(ies), and in case of minors, including their parents or guardians, as the court may deem fit;

b. Training, seminars, and lectures on:

i. Anger Management Skills;

ii. Problem-solving and/or Conflict Resolution Skills;

iii. Values Formation;

c. Payment for cost of treatment

The program may be administered by duly licensed private therapists and counselors. For indigents, the court may direct the Mental Health Division to perform the necessary counseling.

--- [Page 13] ---
**SECTION 19. CONFIDENTIALITY OF RECORDS AND PROCEEDINGS**

All records and proceedings involving minors, from initial contact/reporting until the final decision of the case shall be considered privileged and confidential. The public shall be excluded during the proceedings and the records shall not be disclosed directly or indirectly to any one of the parties or participants in the proceedings for any purpose whatsoever. Parties privy to the proceedings are not allowed to disclose any information regarding any proceeding, unless for the purposes of proceedings which are under this Ordinance.

**SECTION 20. IMPLEMENTING RULES AND REGULATIONS**

The City Social Welfare and Development Office (CSWDIO), in conjunction with the CABC, shall take charge in the creation of the Implementing Rules and Regulations of this Ordinance within one hundred twenty (120) days after its effectiveness.

**SECTION 21. INFORMATION DISSEMINATION**

A city-wide awareness campaign on the city's anti-bullying policy, as encapsulated in this Ordinance, shall be implemented using tri-media, on-ground, and online platforms.

Educational institutions are required to include the city's anti-bullying policy in their school orientations, school handbooks, and other information materials. Regular lectures on the prevention of bullying and other bullying-related topics shall be done in educational institutions and workplaces with the aid of organizations like the Philippine Mental Health Association and other similar entities.

**SECTION 22. SUPPLEMENTARY APPLICATION**

Related existing laws on Anti-bullying and Mental Health such as RA No. 7877, or the Anti-Sexual Harassment Act of 1995; RA No. 9262, or the Anti-Violence Against Women and Their Children Act of 2004; RA No. 11313, or the Safe Spaces Act, and other related laws, shall find supplementary application.

**SECTION 23. SEPARABILITY CLAUSE**

If, for any reason, any section or provision of this Ordinance shall be declared unconstitutional or invalid, other sections or provisions hereof not affected by such declaration shall continue to be in full force and effect.

**SECTION 24. REPEALING CLAUSE**

All Orders, Resolutions, Executive Orders, Memoranda, and Administrative Regulations or part thereof in conflict or inconsistent with the provisions of this Ordinance are thereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly.

**SECTION 25. EFFECTIVITY**

This Ordinance shall take effect immediately upon publication in the manner provided by law.
