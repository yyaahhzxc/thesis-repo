\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Under statutory preemption in \textit{Magtajas v. Pryce Properties} and Republic Act No.~7160, local ordinances cannot contradict superior national statutes. Manual ex-ante reviews in the Davao City Sangguniang Panlungsod strain researchers under statutory volume and archival scanning noise. To assist screening within local computing limits, this study presents an offline coarse-to-fine semantic conflict detection system decoupling candidate retrieval from cross-attention inference. Stage~1 fuses Okapi BM25 and dense bi-encoder embeddings via Reciprocal Rank Fusion over 27,248 enactments and 177,423 provisions; it attains 100.0\% Recall@50 on a controlled benchmark and 65.14\% across the 25,432 national statutes under unassisted open-corpus retrieval. Stage~2 evaluates candidate pairs using a fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3 cross-encoder with Knowledge Graph thresholding ($\tau^*(u,v)$) and numeric-bound verification; ablations showed that unfiltered 1-hop Stage~1 graph expansion lowered specificity without improving recall, so graph signals operate only in Stage~2. Under zero-leakage premise-grouped cross-validation across 22 premises from 17 statutes on a 350-pair faculty adviser-reviewed benchmark (198 unique instances), the model achieves $86.44\% \pm 6.05\%$ accuracy and 0.8945 Conflict $F_1$. Across six full-length draft ordinances (52 sections), open-corpus screening detects all 7 planted conflicts alongside 4 false positives and clears 41 of 45 compliant sections at 91.11\% specificity (0.7778 Conflict $F_1$, 0.8974 $F_2$). Across 10 authentic Supreme Court and municipal ordinances, full-PDF screening flags all 5 invalidated enactments. Algorithmic optimizations cut full-document audit latency by 54.9\% to 12.69~seconds on an entry-level CPU using 1,416.4~MB of memory. An offline web prototype provides explainable preemption alerts as an ex-ante decision-support baseline for Philippine local legislative review.
