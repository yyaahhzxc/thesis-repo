\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Under the constitutional doctrine of statutory preemption established in \textit{Magtajas v. Pryce Properties} and Section~5(a) of Republic Act No.~7160 (the Local Government Code of 1991), local government ordinances in the Philippines are strictly subordinate to national statutes. An ordinance that prohibits an activity authorized by national law or authorizes an activity prohibited by national law is \textit{ultra vires} and void. In the Davao City Sangguniang Panlungsod, local legislative staff face substantial administrative and cognitive burdens when conducting manual ex-ante consistency reviews of proposed draft ordinances against decades of national laws and existing local measures. This challenge is intensified by the volume of positive law and the severe optical character recognition (OCR) degradation prevalent in historical local scanning archives.

To address these challenges, this study presents a coarse-to-fine semantic conflict detection architecture specifically designed for ex-ante legislative review and deployable within standard local government computing environments. The system decouples broad statutory candidate discovery from compute-intensive logical inference into a two-stage retrieve-then-entail pipeline. In Stage~1 (Coarse Candidate Retrieval), hybrid search combining sparse lexical matching (Okapi BM25) and dense neural embeddings (transformer bi-encoders) fused through Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) with a statutory hierarchy priority safeguard searches a unified dual-corpus repository of 27,096 enactments (176,421 searchable provisions) to isolate a compact shortlist of top-$k$ candidate provisions ($k \le 50$). In Stage~2 (Fine Conflict Detection), candidate statute-ordinance pairs are classified through fine-tuned transformer cross-encoders into three directional normative categories: Contradiction, Entailment, or Neutral. High-risk contradictions are isolated using calibrated, cost-sensitive decision thresholds ($\tau^* = 0.45$) and explained through intrinsic token-level self-attention heatmaps.

The computational framework was evaluated across a 350-pair faculty adviser-verified ground truth benchmark stratified across eight Macro Legal Domains and three difficulty tiers, alongside authentic Supreme Court jurisprudential cases. Empirical findings demonstrate that the hybrid Stage~1 retrieval pipeline captures 100\% of controlling statutory preemption provisions on landmark legal cases. In Stage~2, the fine-tuned \texttt{DeBERTa-v3-base-NLI} cross-encoder achieved a 5-fold cross-validation accuracy of 88.30\% $\pm$ 3.44\% and a Conflict $F_1$-score of 0.9008 $\pm$ 0.0421, significantly outperforming canonical supervised baselines (McNemar's $\chi^2 = 4.27, p = 0.0388$) and unadapted zero-shot models while eliminating affirmative entailment bias. Algorithmic optimizations---including procedural boilerplate filtering and dynamic token padding---accelerated full-document audit throughput by $5.79\times$, enabling complete draft ordinance screening in 12.68~seconds on a quad-core desktop CPU with a 1,416.4~MB resident memory footprint. A single-page web prototype was developed to deliver explainable, real-time preemption alerts, establishing an operational baseline for low-resource Philippine legal informatics and providing an assistive decision-support tool for local legislative drafting.
