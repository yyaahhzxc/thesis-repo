\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Under statutory preemption in \textit{Magtajas v. Pryce Properties} and Republic Act No.~7160, local ordinances cannot contradict superior national statutes. In the Davao City Sangguniang Panlungsod, manual ex-ante reviews strain researchers due to statutory volume and archival scanning noise. To assist screening within local computing limits, this study presents an offline coarse-to-fine semantic conflict detection system decoupling candidate retrieval from cross-attention inference. Stage~1 fuses Okapi BM25 and dense bi-encoder embeddings via Reciprocal Rank Fusion over 27,248 enactments and 177,423 provisions; it attains 100.0\% Recall@50 on a controlled benchmark and 65.14\% across the 25,432 national statutes under unassisted open-corpus retrieval. Stage~2 evaluates candidate pairs using a fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3 cross-encoder with calibrated thresholds. Under zero-leakage premise-grouped cross-validation across 22 premises from 17 statutes on a 350-pair faculty adviser-reviewed benchmark (198 unique instances), the model achieves $86.44\% \pm 6.05\%$ accuracy and 0.8945 Conflict $F_1$. In an open-corpus pilot across six draft ordinances, the pipeline flagged two of seven planted conflicts with six false alarms while clearing 39 of 45 compliant sections at 86.67\% specificity. With oracle candidates, it detected all seven planted conflicts, which confirms Stage~1 retrieval as the operational constraint. Algorithmic optimizations cut full-document audit latency by 54.9\% to 12.69~seconds on an entry-level CPU using 1,416.4~MB of memory. An offline web prototype provides explainable preemption alerts as an ex-ante decision-support baseline for Philippine local legislative review.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Semantic Conflict Detection, Ex-Ante Legislative Review, Statutory Preemption, Natural Language Inference, Information Retrieval, Cross-Encoder, Local Ordinances, Sangguniang Panlungsod, Davao City.
