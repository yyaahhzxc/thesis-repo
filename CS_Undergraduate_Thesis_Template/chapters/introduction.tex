\chapter{Introduction}
\section{Background of the Study}
A functional legal system requires strict consistency so that citizens can clearly understand their rights and obligations~\cite{fuller1964the}. However, the sheer volume of modern legislation has grown so rapidly that human practitioners can no longer track every active rule~\cite{ruhl2015measuring, chalkidis2022lexglue}. As governing bodies continuously pass new regulations to address emerging issues, they frequently enact laws that inadvertently contradict older ones~\cite{sartor2005legal, kelsen1967pure}. Because the total number of documents is large, identifying these hidden conflicts through manual review is slow and mentally taxing for legal staff. This issue is particularly severe in local government units, where historical ordinances are often kept in scattered files alongside fragmented digital governance infrastructure~\cite{conchas2025digital}.

Within the Philippine legal framework, local governments like Davao City operate under delegated authority and can only enact laws permitted by the national government~\cite{philgov1991ra7160}. Due to this structural limitation, the Magtajas v. Pryce Doctrine strictly prohibits any local ordinance from contradicting established national statutes~\cite{scp1994magtajasvpryce}. Consequently, in the local context, city councils must rigorously audit every newly proposed draft ordinance to ensure it logically aligns with existing laws before final approval. If reviewers overlook a normative conflict, such as a local ordinance prohibiting an activity that a national law explicitly permits, the resulting local legislation is legally void. Passing invalid laws triggers jurisdictional disputes and wastes administrative resources. It can also compromise the constitutional rights of the public.

To address these legal risks, the legislative process requires an ex-ante evaluation, drawn from the Latin for `before the event.' Unlike an `ex-post' review, which occurs after an enactment takes effect and causes legal harm, an ex-ante audit provides a preventative first line of defense. By checking a proposed ordinance for conflicts while it is still in the drafting phase, the Sangguniang Panlungsod can proactively align local rules with national statutes. This preventative approach is consistent with modern Philippine standards like Republic Act No.~11032 or the Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of 2018, which encourages government agencies to perform regulatory assessments to ensure that new rules do not create unnecessary or conflicting burdens on the public.

The decentralized drafting process of local ordinances further complicates manual review because the resulting texts are often highly unstructured~\cite{spdc2022ra00122}. City councils frequently splice and amend different drafts, producing verbose documents that blend formal legal English with Cebuano and Filipino expressions~\cite{dreisbach2021language}. Furthermore, because local archives heavily rely on digitized PDF scans, the text suffers from severe Optical Character Recognition (OCR) errors. While OCR is typically 98--99 percent accurate for clean English text, studies demonstrate that recognition accuracy degrades substantially for low-resource languages~\cite{ignat2022ocr}, with OCR on Cebuano text encountering a sampled word error rate of 6.9 percent~\cite{oard2003desperately}. These errors cause an ``error cascade'' that corrupts standard vocabulary. As a result, traditional search engines struggle to distinguish between a statutory obligation and a strict prohibition.

In light of these challenges, the proponents aim to develop an assistive screening application that retrieves relevant national and local laws and compares them against proposed Davao City ordinances to detect potential conflicts. Because legal interpretation remains the responsibility of trained legislative staff, the system is designed as a decision-support aid rather than an autonomous legal adjudicator. Running this screening workflow locally on standard office hardware would allow the Sangguniang Panlungsod to check draft texts before formal passage without exposing sensitive legislative drafts to external cloud services. The following sections present the problem statement, objectives, scope, and definitions that guide this study.


\section{Problem Statement}
This study investigates the use of a multi-stage computational architecture to assist legal staff in auditing proposed local legislation in Davao City. Specifically, the study aims to answer the following research questions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item What are the specific linguistic parameters and structural markers that define a ``semantic conflict'' between a proposed local ordinance and existing local or national laws?
    \item How can a Coarse-to-Fine system be designed to efficiently retrieve relevant national and local statutes before performing deep logical inference on constrained LGU hardware?
    \item Which combination of hybrid Information Retrieval (IR) algorithms and Natural Language Inference (NLI) models provides the highest accuracy when processing the specific hierarchy of Philippine legal texts?
    \item What is the empirical performance of the proposed pipeline when evaluated against a manually curated Ground Truth dataset of local and national conflict pairs?
    \item How usable and viable is the final system interface for key stakeholders within the Davao City Sangguniang Panlungsod?
\end{itemize}

\section{Objectives of the Study}
The primary goal of this research is to develop a computationally efficient, two-stage semantic conflict detection system for proposed Davao City ordinances. Specifically, this study aims to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Identify the specific linguistic characteristics and structural constraints of Davao City's legislative archive that contribute to semantic ambiguity and retrieval failure;
    \item Design a Coarse-to-Fine computational pipeline that decouples document retrieval from logical inference to optimize resource usage for local deployment;
    \item Implement a hybrid Information Retrieval (IR) algorithm and Natural Language Inference (NLI) system specifically calibrated for the Philippine legal hierarchy;
    \item Evaluate the system's reliability by comparing its automated detections against a faculty adviser-reviewed ground truth benchmark of known legal conflicts to measure how accurately it identifies contradictions; and
    \item Develop an offline web interface for the Davao City Sangguniang Panlungsod to accept uploaded draft documents and present explainable conflict alerts, while profiling runtime efficiency and preparing an institutional evaluation framework for legislative researchers.
\end{itemize}


\section{Significance of the Study}
The Davao City Sangguniang Panlungsod is the primary institutional beneficiary of this consistency checking system. Moving from manual statutory review to computational screening can save legislative staff substantial time in cross-referencing multi-volume legislative digests. This screening tool helps the council identify legally vulnerable measures before enactment and reduces the risk of judicial invalidation or administrative deadlocks under statutory preemption. It gives the local legislative council a faster and more consistent way to vet regulatory drafts. In practice, the system integrates automated checks into pre-plenary committee review and Second Reading preparation so drafters can resolve statutory conflicts during floor amendments.

The study provides substantial operational value to legislative staff, particularly legal researchers and committee bill drafters. In contemporary local legislative practice, legal researchers must examine extensive regulatory provisions across disparate national codes without centralized semantic indexing tools. The proposed system provides an automated cross-statutory audit trail, pinpointing candidate national statutes and fellow city ordinances that govern the same regulatory sphere. By surfacing relevant provisions and generating token-level attention attribution heatmaps, the architecture is designed to reduce reviewer fatigue and reading oversight during high-volume legislative sessions. Consequently, legal officers can conduct evidence-grounded preliminary reviews and focus their analytical expertise on high-level statutory interpretation.

Beyond Davao City, this research offers a practical offline deployment model for other Local Government Units (LGUs) and oversight agencies such as the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG). In compliance with the Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of 2018 (Republic Act No.~11032), local governments are mandated to perform systematic Regulatory Impact Assessments to ensure local regulations do not create redundant or conflicting business burdens. Running an automated consistency checking pipeline on standard office desktop computers avoids recurring cloud subscriptions and specialized computing clusters. This design shows how budget-constrained local offices can run neural screening tools locally while keeping draft legislation private.

The research also contributes baseline resources and empirical benchmarks to the Philippine Natural Language Processing and legal informatics community. Prior domestic legal technology research concentrated predominantly on national appellate case summarization and judicial outcome forecasting, whereas local statutory conflict detection remained largely unaddressed. This investigation establishes an empirical baseline for processing localized Philippine statutory corpora marked by historical scanning noise and regional drafting syntax. It also evaluates whether compact cross-encoders can handle affirmative entailment bias and directional statutory contradictions on resource-constrained local hardware.

Finally, the general public and the local business community benefit directly through the adoption of clearer, legally consistent public policy. When local ordinances clash with superior national statutes, citizens and local enterprises face legal uncertainty through conflicting compliance directives or unauthorized penalties. As an ex-ante procedural safety net, the system helps ensure that enacted city ordinances harmonize with national standards and existing local legislation. This statutory coherence strengthens institutional transparency and reinforces the local rule of law.


\section{Scope and Limitations}
The scope of this study focuses on developing an automated semantic conflict detection system to evaluate draft ordinances from Davao City against a dual statutory search space. This collection encompasses national statutory enactments compiled to the extent accessible from public legal repositories, paired alongside the historical archive of enacted Davao City local ordinances collected to the fullest extent possible from local legislative records. Operationally, the system audits draft measures across two complementary dimensions: vertical statutory preemption, which checks whether local provisions conflict with superior national statutes under the \textit{Magtajas} doctrine and the delegated limits of Republic Act No.~7160 (specifically Sections~16 and~458); and horizontal consistency, which checks that proposed clauses do not contradict or inadvertently repeal active local ordinances. While the dual-corpus retrieval index and prototype architecture ingest both statutory tiers to support horizontal as well as vertical searches, formal empirical benchmark evaluations in this study focus strictly on vertical statutory preemption against superior national statutes. Systematic horizontal benchmark construction is left as a recommendation for future work.

Several practical boundaries define the scope of this research. The statutory knowledge base is limited to codified national legislative instruments and enacted local ordinances. To preserve retrieval index compactness and prevent litigation records from distorting similarity rankings, judicial case law from Supreme Court decisions and departmental administrative circulars are excluded from the primary index. Furthermore, the local ordinance corpus reflects local legislative record-keeping constraints, where recent council terms benefit from digital database records while earlier decades rely on curated landmark ordinances due to physical archive gaps. The system evaluates drafts against the combined corpus gathered through public portals and direct institutional coordination with the Sangguniang Panlungsod. Comprehensive optical character recovery of older paper files remains an ongoing archival task.

The system operates exclusively on English-language enactments pursuant to Section~20, Chapter~5, Book~I of Executive Order No.~292, where the English text controls in formal statutory interpretation. While local drafts occasionally incorporate Cebuano or Filipino phrases, restricting the primary reasoning pipeline to English prevents semantic drift and avoids translation distortion. At the inference tier, the system evaluates proposed ordinance sections against retrieved candidate provisions using asymmetric span-level pairing, matching one retrieved statutory provision against a multi-sentence draft ordinance block ($1\text{-vs-}N$ sentence span pairing). The pipeline excludes multi-hop reasoning across multiple independent statutes simultaneously. Detecting intricate legal contradictions that emerge only when synthesizing two or more disparate national statutes exceeds the computational capacity of standard workstation hardware and is designated as a recommendation for future research.

The computational architecture is intentionally restricted to open-weight transformer encoder models capable of local deployment on standard Local Government Unit (LGU) desktop computers, specifically targeting sub-600M parameter models requiring less than 2~GB of inference VRAM. Massive autoregressive large language models and proprietary cloud APIs are excluded to maintain institutional data privacy and support reliable offline operation without recurring cloud fees. Within this architecture, the system operates strictly as an assistive decision-support tool providing interpretable outputs, such as attention heatmaps and calibrated confidence scores, without rewriting or generating statutory text. Finally, the operational scope of the inference engine is limited to direct normative and modal contradictions arising from explicit textual collisions, such as local prohibitions of nationally authorized activities or penalty ceiling infractions. The framework does not model longitudinal legislative histories or multi-year financial calculations across unprovided statutory baselines, as observed in complex judicial tax disputes~\cite{scp2017mindanaoshopping}. Modeling such multi-hop temporal construction and arithmetic accounting exceeds the ex-ante scope of cross-encoder screening and is preserved as an avenue for specialized fiscal analytics.


\section{Definition of Terms}
\begin{description}
    \item[Bi-Encoder.] A dual-tower transformer architecture that maps queries and candidate statutory provisions independently into a shared dense vector embedding space. This independent encoding allows fast semantic similarity search via vector dot products or cosine distance.

    \item[Coarse-to-Fine Architecture.] A two-stage decoupled computational framework that pairs a broad high-throughput candidate retrieval module (coarse stage) with a compute-intensive pairwise sequence classification model (fine stage) to manage extensive textual collections on bounded hardware.

    \item[Contradiction.] A directional NLI classification label indicating that the truth of the hypothesis is incompatible with the regulatory mandate of the premise, which signals a direct normative conflict or statutory collision.

    \item[Cross-Encoder.] A single-tower transformer architecture that ingests concatenated premise-hypothesis pairs into a unified self-attention matrix so that tokens across both texts attend to each other directly during semantic classification.

    \item[Entailment.] An NLI classification label indicating that the substantive directive of the hypothesis follows from or is explicitly authorized by the governing statutory premise.

    \item[Ex-Ante Legislative Review.] The proactive evaluation of proposed ordinances or administrative rules prior to formal enactment to detect statutory collisions while the draft remains open to revision.

    \item[Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI).] Computational methods that provide human-interpretable rationales for machine learning decisions; in this system, implemented through token-level attention attribution heatmaps that highlight conflicting text spans.

    \item[Fleiss' Kappa ($\kappa$).] A statistical measure of inter-annotator agreement among three or more raters assessing categorical labels, used in this study to measure consensus reliability across legal annotators.

    \item[Horizontal Inconsistency.] A normative clash or unintended implied repeal occurring between a newly drafted local ordinance and an existing local ordinance enacted within the same legislative jurisdiction.

    \item[Information Retrieval (IR).] The computational discipline and algorithmic process of searching and ranking relevant candidate documents or legal provisions from extensive unstructured text collections in response to a query.

    \item[Local Ordinances.] Enacted legislative measures promulgated by the Davao City Sangguniang Panlungsod under devolved legislative powers that carry the force and effect of law within the territorial jurisdiction of the city.

    \item[Magtajas v. Pryce Doctrine.] The Philippine constitutional doctrine establishing that local government ordinances are subordinate to national statutes; any ordinance that permits what an Act of Congress forbids, or forbids what an Act of Congress permits, is \textit{ultra vires} and void.

    \item[Natural Language Inference (NLI).] A core natural language processing task that evaluates the directional semantic relationship between a premise text and a hypothesis text across three categorical classes: Entailment, Contradiction, or Neutral.

    \item[Neutral.] An NLI classification label indicating that the truth or enforcement of the hypothesis neither follows from nor contradicts the regulatory command of the statutory premise. This label signals thematic and regulatory independence between the two provisions.

    \item[Normative Conflict.] In legal theory and formal deontic logic, a condition where two or more legally valid enactments impose mutually incompatible obligations, prohibitions, or permissions upon the same subject under identical circumstances.

    \item[Optical Character Recognition (OCR) Noise.] Textual distortions such as typographical errors and broken tokens introduced when physical paper documents are scanned and converted into machine-readable text.

    \item[Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF).] An unsupervised rank aggregation algorithm that merges ranked candidate lists from disparate retrieval systems (such as lexical BM25 and dense bi-encoders) based on reciprocal rank positions without requiring calibrated score normalization.

    \item[Sangguniang Panlungsod (SP).] The unicameral legislative council of Davao City, vested by Republic Act No.~7160 with the corporate and police authority to enact local ordinances and approve resolutions.

    \item[Semantic Conflict Detection.] The computational formulation and automated identification of directional normative contradictions between legislative provisions using hybrid information retrieval and natural language inference.

    \item[Statutory Preemption.] The constitutional and administrative principle under which superior national legislation invalidates or limits the regulatory scope of local enactments governing the same subject matter.

    \item[Vision-Language Model (VLM).] A multimodal deep learning architecture that combines visual processing backbones with transformer language decoders to transcribe document page images into structured text while preserving layout and alphanumeric precision.
\end{description}
