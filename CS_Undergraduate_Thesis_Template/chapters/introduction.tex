\chapter{Introduction}
\section{Background of the Study}
A functional legal system requires strict consistency so that citizens can clearly understand their rights and obligations \cite{fuller1964the}. However, the sheer volume of modern legislation has grown so rapidly that human practitioners can no longer track every active rule \cite{ruhl2015measuring, chalkidis2022lexglue}. As governing bodies continuously pass new regulations to address emerging issues, they frequently enact laws that inadvertently contradict older ones \cite{sartor2005legal, kelsen1967pure}. Because the total number of documents is overwhelming, identifying these hidden conflicts through manual review is computationally impossible for legal staff. This issue is particularly severe in local government units, where historical ordinances are often scattered across fragmented files rather than maintained in a structured digital database \cite{conchas2025digital}.

Within the Philippine legal framework, local governments like Davao City operate under delegated authority and can only enact laws permitted by the national government \cite{philgov1991ra7160}. Due to this structural limitation, the Magtajas v. Pryce Doctrine strictly prohibits any local ordinance from contradicting established national statutes \cite{scp1994magtajasvpryce}. Consequently, in the local context, city councils must rigorously audit every newly proposed draft ordinance to ensure it logically aligns with existing laws before final approval. If reviewers overlook a normative conflict, such as a local ordinance prohibiting an activity that a national law explicitly permits, the resulting local legislation is legally void. Passing invalid laws creates severe jurisdictional disputes, wastes administrative resources, and ultimately compromises the constitutional rights of the public.

To address these legal risks, the legislative process must utilize an ex-ante evaluation: a term derived from Latin meaning 'before the event.' Unlike an 'ex-post' review, which happens after a law is already in effect and often after legal damage has occurred, an ex-ante audit acts as a first line of defense. By checking a proposed ordinance for conflicts while it is still in the drafting phase, the Sangguniang Panlungsod can proactively align local rules with national statutes. This preventative approach is consistent with modern Philippine standards like Republic Act No. 11032 or the Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of 2018, which encourages government agencies to perform regulatory assessments to ensure that new rules do not create unnecessary or conflicting burdens on the public.

The decentralized drafting process of local ordinances further complicates manual review because the resulting texts are often highly unstructured \cite{spdc2022ra00122}. City councils frequently splice and amend different drafts, producing verbose documents that blend formal legal English with regional dialects like Cebuano and administrative "Taglish" \cite{dreisbach2021language}. Furthermore, because local archives heavily rely on digitized PDF scans, the text suffers from severe Optical Character Recognition (OCR) errors. While OCR is typically 98--99 percent accurate for clean English text, studies demonstrate that recognition accuracy degrades substantially for low-resource languages \cite{ignat2022ocr}, with rapid lexicon extraction for Cebuano specifically encountering word error rates of 6.9 percent \cite{oard2003desperately}. These errors cause an ``error cascade'' that shatters standard vocabulary, making it difficult for traditional search engines to semantically differentiate between a statutory obligation and a strict prohibition.

In light of these challenges, the proponents seek to develop a specialized application capable of retrieving relevant legal documents and comparing them against proposed ordinances to detect potential conflicts.


\section{Problem Statement}
This study is conducted to investigate the use of a multi-stage architecture to automate the manual auditing of proposed local legislation in Davao City. 

Specifically, the study aims to answer the following research questions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item What are the specific linguistic parameters and structural markers that define a "semantic conflict" between a proposed local ordinance and existing local or national laws?
    \item How can a Coarse-to-Fine system be designed to efficiently retrieve relevant national and local statutes before performing deep logical inference on constrained LGU hardware?
    \item Which combination of a hybrid Information Retrieval (IR) algorithms and Natural Language Inference (NLI) models provides the highest accuracy when processing the specific hierarchy of Philippine legal texts?
    \item What is the empirical performance of the proposed pipeline when evaluated against a manually curated Ground Truth dataset of local and national conflict pairs?
    \item How usable and viable is the final system interface for key stakeholders within the Davao City Sangguniang Panlungsod?
\end{itemize}

\section{Objectives of the Study}
The primary goal of this research is to develop a computationally efficient, two-stage semantic conflict detection system for proposed Davao City ordinances. 

Specifically, this study aims to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Identify the specific linguistic characteristics and structural constraints of Davao City’s legislative archive that contribute to semantic ambiguity and retrieval failure;
    \item Design a Coarse-to-Fine computational pipeline that decouples document retrieval from logical inference to optimize resource usage for local deployment;
    \item Implement a hybrid Information Retrieval (IR) algorithm and Natural Language Inference (NLI) system specifically calibrated for the Philippine legal hierarchy;
    \item Evaluate the system’s reliability by comparing its automated detections against a verified set of known legal conflicts to measure how accurately it identifies contradictions; and
    \item Develop a user-friendly web interface for the Davao City Sangguniang Panlungsod designed to accept uploaded draft documents and provide real-time conflict alerts.
\end{itemize}


\section{Significance of the Study}
The Davao City Sangguniang Panlungsod stands as the primary institutional beneficiary of this automated consistency checking system. By transitioning from manual statutory review to computational auditing, the tool saves legislative staff hundreds of hours of manual cross-referencing across multi-volume legislative digests. This computational intervention actively prevents the city from enacting legally vulnerable measures that invite judicial invalidation, public injunctions, or administrative deadlocks under the doctrine of statutory preemption. It equips the local legislative council with the operational capacity to draft regulatory measures with a heightened degree of legal certainty and procedural speed. Ultimately, the system modernizes the local legislative lifecycle by embedding rigorous logical safeguards directly into the pre-plenary committee review and Second Reading preparation phase, equipping drafters to resolve statutory conflicts during the floor amendment period.

The study provides substantial operational value to legal researchers, committee secretaries, and legislative bill drafters. In contemporary local legislative practice, legal researchers must examine extensive regulatory provisions across disparate national codes without centralized semantic indexing tools. The proposed system provides an automated cross-statutory audit trail, pinpointing candidate national statutes and fellow city ordinances that govern the same regulatory sphere. By surfacing relevant provisions and generating token-level self-attention attribution heatmaps, the architecture substantially mitigates cognitive fatigue and reading oversight during high-volume legislative sessions. Consequently, legal officers can conduct evidence-grounded preliminary reviews and focus their analytical expertise on high-level statutory interpretation.

Beyond the immediate city council, this research establishes an edge-deployable computational blueprint for Local Government Units (LGUs) nationwide and oversight agencies such as the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG). In compliance with the Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of 2018 (Republic Act No.~11032), local governments are mandated to perform systematic Regulatory Impact Assessments to ensure local regulations do not create redundant or conflicting business burdens. Demonstrating that an automated consistency checking pipeline operates efficiently on standard consumer-grade office desktop computers proves that advanced artificial intelligence can be deployed within budget-constrained local government environments without recurring cloud subscriptions or specialized computing clusters.

The research also contributes foundational advancements to the Philippine Natural Language Processing and legal informatics community. Prior domestic legal technology research has concentrated predominantly on national appellate court case summarization and judicial outcome forecasting, leaving local statutory conflict detection largely unaddressed. This investigation establishes an empirical baseline for processing low-resource, localized Philippine statutory corpora characterized by multi-generational scanning noise, administrative Taglish, and specialized legislative syntax. Furthermore, the empirical findings demonstrate that lightweight, fine-tuned cross-encoders can effectively overcome affirmative entailment bias and resolve directional statutory contradictions on resource-constrained local hardware.

Finally, the general public and the local business community benefit directly through the promulgation of coherent, constitutionally sound public policy. When local ordinances clash with superior national statutes, citizens and local enterprises bear the brunt of legal uncertainty, facing conflicting compliance directives or unauthorized penal sanctions. By serving as an ex-ante procedural safety net, the system helps ensure that enacted city ordinances harmonize with national standards and existing local legislation. This statutory coherence fosters institutional transparency, strengthens regulatory predictability, and upholds the integrity of the local rule of law.


\section{Scope and Limitations}
The scope of this study focuses on developing an automated semantic conflict detection system to evaluate draft ordinances from Davao City against a dual statutory search space. This collection encompasses national statutory enactments compiled to the extent accessible from public legal repositories, paired alongside the historical archive of enacted Davao City local ordinances collected to the fullest extent possible from local legislative records. Operationally, the system audits draft measures across two complementary dimensions: vertical statutory preemption, ensuring local provisions do not contradict superior national statutes under the \textit{Magtajas} doctrine and Republic Act No.~7160; and horizontal consistency, verifying that proposed clauses do not contradict, duplicate, or inadvertently cause implied repeals of active local ordinances.

To establish an administratively grounded and computationally feasible engineering boundary, several explicit delimitations govern this research. The statutory knowledge base is strictly bounded to codified national legislative instruments and enacted local ordinances. To preserve retrieval index compactness and prevent ambiguous litigation records from distorting similarity rankings, judicial case law from Supreme Court decisions and departmental administrative circulars are excluded from the primary index. Furthermore, the local ordinance corpus reflects the practical realities of local legislative record-keeping, where recent council terms benefit from digital database records while earlier decades rely on curated landmark ordinances due to physical archive gaps. The system evaluates drafts strictly against the combined corpus gathered through public portals and direct institutional coordination with the Sangguniang Panlungsod, leaving the comprehensive optical character recovery of older paper files as an ongoing archival task.

The linguistic scope of the system operates exclusively on English-language enactments, following Section~20, Chapter~5, Book~I of Executive Order No.~292, which establishes that the English text controls in formal statutory interpretation. While local drafts occasionally incorporate regional Cebuano phrases or administrative Taglish, restricting the primary reasoning pipeline to English prevents semantic drift and avoids translation distortion. At the inference tier, the system evaluates proposed ordinance sections against retrieved candidate provisions using asymmetric span-level pairing ($1\text{-vs-}N$). The pipeline deliberately excludes multi-hop reasoning across multiple independent statutes simultaneously. Detecting intricate legal contradictions that emerge only when synthesizing two or more disparate national statutes exceeds the computational capacity of standard workstation hardware and is designated as a recommendation for future research.

The computational architecture is intentionally restricted to an open-weight transformer encoder envelope capable of local deployment on standard Local Government Unit (LGU) desktop computers, specifically targeting sub-600M parameter models requiring less than 2~GB of inference VRAM. Massive autoregressive large language models and proprietary cloud APIs are excluded to maintain institutional data privacy, avoid recurring cloud API expenditures, and guarantee reliable offline operation. Within this architecture, the system operates strictly as a diagnostic decision-support tool providing transparent, white-box explainability, such as attention heatmaps and calibrated confidence scores, without rewriting or generating statutory text. Finally, the operational scope of the inference engine is delimited to direct normative and modal contradictions arising from explicit textual collisions, such as local prohibitions of federally authorized activities, unauthorized procedural preconditions, or penalty ceiling infractions. The framework deliberately does not model longitudinal legislative histories, historical tax rate compounding, or multi-year financial calculations across unprovided statutory baselines, as observed in complex judicial tax disputes~\cite{scp2017mindanaoshopping}. Modeling such multi-hop temporal construction and arithmetic accounting exceeds the ex-ante diagnostic scope of cross-encoder architectures and is preserved as an avenue for specialized fiscal analytics.


\section{Definition of Terms}
\begin{description}
    \item[Bi-Encoder.] A dual-tower transformer architecture that maps queries and candidate statutory provisions independently into a shared dense vector embedding space, enabling rapid semantic similarity search via vector dot products or cosine distance.

    \item[Coarse-to-Fine Architecture.] A two-stage decoupled computational framework that pairs a high-throughput, broad candidate retrieval module (coarse stage) with a compute-intensive pairwise sequence classification model (fine stage) to manage extensive textual collections on bounded hardware.

    \item[Contradiction.] In Natural Language Inference, a directional semantic relationship where the truth of the hypothesis is incompatible with the regulatory mandate of the premise, indicating a direct normative conflict or statutory collision.

    \item[Cross-Encoder.] A single-tower transformer architecture that ingests concatenated premise-hypothesis pairs into a unified self-attention matrix, enabling full bidirectional cross-attention across all tokens for granular semantic classification.

    \item[Entailment.] In Natural Language Inference, a relationship where the substantive directive of the hypothesis naturally follows from, conforms with, or is explicitly authorized by the governing statutory premise.

    \item[Ex-Ante Legislative Review.] The proactive legal and technical evaluation of proposed legislative drafts, ordinances, or administrative rules prior to their formal enactment, designed to detect statutory collisions and constitutional defects while the text remains amenable to revision.

    \item[Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI).] Computational methods that provide human-interpretable rationales for machine learning decisions; in this system, operationalized through token-level self-attention attribution heatmaps highlighting specific conflicting text spans.

    \item[Fleiss' Kappa ($\kappa$).] A statistical measure of inter-annotator agreement among three or more raters assessing categorical data, utilized to validate consensus reliability across the fifteen Sangguniang Panlungsod legal researchers.

    \item[Horizontal Inconsistency.] A normative clash, redundancy, or unintended implied repeal occurring between a newly drafted local ordinance and an existing, fellow local ordinance enacted within the same legislative jurisdiction.

    \item[Information Retrieval (IR).] The computational discipline and algorithmic process of searching, filtering, and ranking relevant candidate documents or legal provisions from extensive unstructured text collections in response to a query.

    \item[Local Ordinances.] Enacted legislative measures promulgated by the Davao City Sangguniang Panlungsod under devolved legislative powers that carry the force and effect of law within the territorial jurisdiction of the city.

    \item[Magtajas v. Pryce Doctrine.] The foundational Philippine constitutional principle establishing that local government ordinances are strictly subordinate to national statutes, rendering any ordinance that permits what an Act of Congress forbids, or forbids what an Act of Congress permits, \textit{ultra vires} and void.

    \item[Natural Language Inference (NLI).] A core natural language processing task that evaluates the directional semantic relationship between a premise text and a hypothesis text, categorizing the pair into Entailment, Contradiction, or Neutral.

    \item[Neutral.] In Natural Language Inference, a relationship where the truth or enforcement of the hypothesis neither follows from nor contradicts the regulatory command of the statutory premise, indicating thematic or regulatory independence.

    \item[Normative Conflict.] In legal theory and formal deontic logic, a condition where two or more legally valid enactments impose mutually incompatible obligations, prohibitions, or permissions upon the same subject under identical circumstances.

    \item[Optical Character Recognition (OCR) Noise.] Textual distortions, typographical errors, broken character tokens, and structural formatting anomalies introduced when physical paper documents are scanned and converted into machine-readable text.

    \item[Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF).] An unsupervised rank aggregation algorithm that merges ranked candidate lists from disparate retrieval systems (such as lexical BM25 and dense bi-encoders) based on reciprocal rank positions without requiring calibrated score normalization.

    \item[Sangguniang Panlungsod (SP).] The unicameral legislative council of Davao City, vested by Republic Act No.~7160 with the corporate and police authority to enact local ordinances and approve resolutions.

    \item[Semantic Conflict Detection.] The computational formulation and automated identification of directional normative contradictions between legislative provisions using continuous vector representations, lexical indexing, and natural language inference models.

    \item[Statutory Preemption.] The constitutional and administrative principle under which superior national legislation invalidates, overrides, or limits the regulatory scope of local enactments governing the same subject matter.

    \item[Vision-Language Model (VLM).] A multimodal deep learning architecture that combines visual processing backbones with transformer language decoders to transcribe document page images into structured text while preserving layout and alphanumeric precision.
\end{description}
