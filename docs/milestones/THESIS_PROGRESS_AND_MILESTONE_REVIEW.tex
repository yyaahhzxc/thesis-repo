\section*{Executive Summary}
Since completing \textbf{Milestone 1 (Data Description --- Machine Learning)} on September 4, 2026, this research has matured from descriptive planning into an empirically benchmarked, reproducible pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Chapter 3 (Methodology) Expanded by >300\%} (179 to 540+ \LaTeX{} lines), chronologically aligned with the machine pipeline, and fortified with formal mathematical specifications, 8 publication figures, and 8 comprehensive empirical tables.
    \item \textbf{Ground Truth 350 Benchmark Finalized ($N=350$)}: Curated across 8 macro legal domains and 3 difficulty tiers with balanced classes (112 Contradictions, 128 Entailments, 110 Neutrals). Packaged into a master 4-sheet review workbook (\url{data/ground_truth_350_adviser_review.xlsx}) with a 15-researcher Sangguniang Panlungsod (SP) panel allocation matrix.
    \item \textbf{Full-Corpus Token Census ($N = 25,432$ laws; 164,620 sections)}: Demonstrated that \textbf{94.83\% of provisions have a median length of 122 tokens} ($\le 512$ tokens). For the 5.17\% tail exceeding 512 tokens, a sentence-aware sliding fallback sub-chunking mechanism was added, alongside candidate evaluation of native long-context \textbf{ModernBERT} (8,192 tokens) versus standard-window \textbf{DeBERTa-v3}.
    \item \textbf{Stage 1 Information Retrieval Empirically Benchmarked}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Full-Corpus Sensitivity ($N = 25,432$ statutes)}: Baseline unconstrained BM25 achieves only 43.14\% Recall@50 on local CPU hardware. Naive hard domain filtering causes catastrophic failure, collapsing recall to 13.43\% due to the pruning of cross-cutting omnibus statutes.
        \item \textit{Candidate Retrieval Ablation ($N = 350$ queries)}: Dense Bi-Encoders (\url{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}) outperform sparse BM25 by \textbf{+17.3\% on Tier 3 latent conflicts} (91.3\% vs 74.0\% Recall@5). \textbf{Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF $k=60$)} reaches \textbf{96.0\% Recall@10} and \textbf{99.7\% Recall@20} in 1.26\,ms CPU latency.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cost-Sensitive Thresholding \& COLIEE 2026 Affirmative Bias Control}: Grounded in official COLIEE 2026 competition proceedings, the pipeline counters neural model affirmative bias (defaulting to Entailment/Neutral) by formalizing asymmetric operational costs ($C(FN) > C(FP)$). Sweeping decision thresholds $\tau \in [0.25, 0.50]$ maximizes the $F_2$-score, prioritizing contradiction recall while maintaining a $P \ge 0.70$ precision floor.
    \item \textbf{Operational End-to-End Prototype}: Implemented in \url{scripts/prototype_pipeline.py}, successfully executing Stage~1 hybrid retrieval, Stage~2 asymmetric NLI sequence classification, and XAI ratio decidendi extraction under the \textit{Magtajas v. Pryce Doctrine}.
    \item \textbf{Document Ingestion Benchmark}: Proved that Vision-Language Models (Qwen2.5-VL and Gemini 1.5 Flash) eliminate the 27.44\% Word Error Rate and 15.64\% vector fidelity degradation of traditional scanner OCR on degraded municipal archives.
\end{enumerate}

Cross-encoding a draft against 25,432 statutes simultaneously requires $\mathcal{O}((L_{\text{ord}} + L_{\text{stat}})^2)$ quadratic memory, instantly exceeding consumer GPU VRAM. Coarse retrieval ($25,432 \rightarrow 50 \rightarrow 1$) eliminates 99.8\% of irrelevant laws in milliseconds.
\end{itemize}

\section{Empirical Experimental Benchmarks}

\subsection{Document Ingestion: Traditional OCR vs. Vision-Language Models}
Tested on representative degraded scanned Davao City ordinances (\textit{Ordinance No. 0667-21}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Traditional Scanner OCR (Baseline)}: 27.44\% Word Error Rate (WER), 25.05\% Character Error Rate (CER), 36.36\% legal marker precision, and a 15.64\% degradation in semantic vector fidelity (84.36\%).
    \item \textbf{Vision-Language Models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B \& Gemini 1.5 Flash)}: \textbf{0.00\% WER}, \textbf{100.0\% legal citation precision}, and \textbf{100.0\% semantic vector fidelity}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Full-Corpus Stage 1 Sensitivity Benchmark (N = 25,432 Statutes)}
Conducted on local CPU workstation evaluating all 350 ground truth queries across the unconstrained database:

\vspace{0.4em}
\noindent\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}Xrrrrrr@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Candidate Retrieval Setup} & \textbf{CPU Latency} & \textbf{R@5} & \textbf{R@10} & \textbf{R@20} & \textbf{R@50} & \textbf{MRR@50} \\

\textit{Key Finding}: Hard domain filtering drops Recall@50 from 43.14\% to 13.43\% because foundational omnibus statutes (e.g., Local Government Code) are pruned. The Hierarchy Priority Safeguard boosts MRR by +45.8\% without recall loss.

\section{Decisions to Verify with Professor \& Adviser}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Decision \arabic*:},leftmargin=6.5em]
    \item \textbf{Exclusion of Generative LLMs}: Core pipeline relies exclusively on deterministic encoder transformers and white-box self-attention heatmaps. Generative LLMs (GPT-4/LLaMA) are excluded to prevent legal hallucination and avoid multi-GPU computing dependencies. \textit{Action: Verify faculty approval of this diagnostic boundary.}
    \item \textbf{Cost-Sensitive Decision Threshold ($\tau^*$) Maximizing $F_2$-Score}: Sweeping contradiction thresholds $\tau \in [0.25, 0.50]$ on validation data to maximize $F_2$, weighting recall twice as heavily as precision to prevent unconstitutional municipal preemption while mitigating affirmative bias. \textit{Action: Confirm faculty approval of $F_2$ threshold tuning over symmetric 0.50 cutoff.}
    \item \textbf{ModernBERT (Native 8,192 Tokens) vs.\ DeBERTa-v3 with Sub-Chunking}: Evaluating whether native long-context attention provides measurable gains on the 5.17\% tail of lengthy provisions compared to compact models paired with sub-chunking. \textit{Action: Confirm candidate Stage~2 model comparison.}
    \item \textbf{Asymmetric Span Pairing ($1\text{-vs-}N$) vs.\ Multi-Hop Graph Reasoning}: Evaluates 1 national section against an $N$-sentence ordinance block. Cross-statutory multi-hop reasoning is reserved for Future Work to preserve LGU CPU feasibility. \textit{Action: Verify undergraduate scope acceptability.}
    \item \textbf{Statutory Knowledge Base Boundary}: National corpus capped at \textbf{25,432 statutory enactments} (Lawphil); judicial case law and department circulars are excluded. \textit{Action: Confirm adequacy of statutory-only corpus.}
    \item \textbf{English-Only Restriction}: Excludes conversational Cebuano and Taglish to prevent tokenizer fragmentation and translation drift. \textit{Action: Confirm English boundary justification.}
    \item \textbf{Static 70/15/15 Split ($N=350$)}: Partitions 245 train, 52 validation, and 53 test pairs (supported by Vabalas et al., 2019 few-shot split guidelines and Card et al., 2020 MDE statistical power) over $k$-fold cross-validation. \textit{Action: Confirm static split acceptability.}
    \item \textbf{SP Human-in-the-Loop Protocol}: 15 legal researchers in 5 sub-panels of 3 evaluating 70 pairs each with a Fleiss' Kappa target $\kappa \ge 0.61$. \textit{Action: Review Excel review workbook and approve endorsement letter.}
    \item \textbf{Rejection of Hard Domain Filtering}: Proved that hard topical filtering drops full-corpus recall to 13.43\%, selecting Reciprocal Rank Fusion instead. \textit{Action: Confirm reporting this empirical failure mode strengthens Chapter 3.}
\end{enumerate}
