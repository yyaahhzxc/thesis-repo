## 2. Objective Academic & Jurisprudential Defense

When defending the identification of these 32 flagged enactments before thesis review panelists or institutional archivists, the proponents rely on three authoritative, non-subjective grounds:

### A. The Physical Technology Shift (Baird, 1993, 2002; Lopresti, 2008)
Over **75.0% (24 enactments)** of all flagged files originated in the pre-2000s, with **65.6% (21 enactments)** enacted under the pre-Local Government Code era (1956–1990). In this period, municipal legislative bodies lacked digital word processing and laser printing. Key physical degradation mechanisms include:
1. **Mechanical Strike Non-Uniformity:** Uneven hammer impact velocity caused ink smudges across closed-loop characters (`e`, `o`, `a`) while leaving letter ascenders and punctuation faint.
2. **Binding & Hole-Punch Occlusions:** Physical binder hole punches create large, dark circular shadow artifacts that directly intersect marginal text and paragraph catchlines.
3. **Underline Baseline Cutting:** Mechanical typewriters created underlines using horizontal underscore strikes (`_`), which directly bisect character descenders (`p`, `g`, `y`, `q`), fragmenting character segmentation in standard OCR.
4. **Carbon Diffusion & Tropical Acidification:** Four to seven decades of physical storage in Davao City's tropical humidity caused ink migration and paper yellowing, superimposing reverse-side text (bleed-through / show-through) onto the front scanning layer.

### B. The Asymmetric Legal Error Cost Principle
Under Section 458 of Republic Act No. 7160 and the *Magtajas v. Pryce Properties* doctrine, municipal penal sanctions are legally invalid if they exceed statutory ceilings (e.g., maximum fines of ₱5,000.00). If an optical engine guesses smudged mechanical typewriter numbers and transcribes `₱100.00` as `₱700.00` or drops a decimal zero, it artificially manufactures an unconstitutional preemption defect. When visual glyphs fall below human legibility thresholds, **an automated model must not guess.** Flagging degraded files for human verification is the only legally defensible action.
