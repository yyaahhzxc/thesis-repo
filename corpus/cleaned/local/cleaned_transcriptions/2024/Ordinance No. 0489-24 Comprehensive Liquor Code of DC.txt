ARTICLE 1. GENERAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE - This Ordinance shall be known as the "The Comprehensive Liquor Code of Davao City."

SECTION 2. DECLARATION OF POLICY - Under Republic Act No. 7160 or the Local Government Code of 1991, the City Government of Davao shall exercise the powers expressly granted, those necessarily implied therefrom, as well as powers necessary, appropriate, and incidental to the exercise thereof. In exercising these powers, the City Government shall act in good faith, with due regard for what are essential to the promotion of the general welfare. Within its respective territorial jurisdiction, it shall ensure and support the maintenance of peace and order, and the preservation of the comfort and convenience of its inhabitants.

Thus, it is the policy of the City Government of Davao to ensure the prevention, suppression, and imposition of proper penalties for drunkenness in public places, and other activities that are inimical to the welfare and morals of the inhabitants of the City. The City shall also regulate the consumption, distribution, sale, and supply of liquor and other alcoholic beverages in the interest of health, economy, peace, and public safety.

SECTION 3. DEFINITION OF TERMS - For purposes of this Ordinance, the following terms are defined operationally:

1. Accommodation Establishments - refer to establishments operating primarily for accommodation purposes including, but not limited to, hotels, resorts, apartment hotels, Mabuhay accommodation establishments (tourist inns, motels, pension houses, bed and breakfast, vacation homes, hostels, and other similar accommodation establishments) private homes used for homestay, ec lodges, serviced apartments, condotels.

2. Alcohol Beverages/Liquor - any drink which contains alcohol as an active agent including, but not limited to, beers, liquors, spirits, vodka, cocktails, wines, locally-distilled alcoholic drinks, such as but not limited to, coconut wine or tuba, lamanbang, basi, and others.

3. Bar or Restobar - shall include any place or establishment whose business includes the sale of alcohol beverages or liquors of any kind to be used or consumed within its premises.

4. Children Rescued - refers to minors rescued from any conceivable forms of danger, including substance use and abuse.

5. Establishments - a collective term used to describe any commercial building, edifice, structure, and the business housed therein.

6. Immediately Dangerous to Life or Health (IDLH) - describes hazards in which acute exposure to the hazard poses a severe risk of immediate health damage, injury, or death. This is determined by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), or the City Health Office (CHO), with proper coordination with FDA, in accordance with their existing policies and standards.

7. Liquor Ban - is the total governmental prohibition of production, transportation, sales, consumption of, or having any transactions or activities involving liquor and/or other alcoholic beverages for a certain period as specified in this Ordinance.

8. Minors – are persons who are below the age of the majority or the legal age of eighteen (18) years, or persons who are eighteen (18) years of age or older but are incapable of taking care of themselves due to their mental incapacity.

9. Motor Vehicle – refers to any land vehicle propelled by any power other than muscular power.

10. 11. Public streets and sidewalks – any public street, highway, bridge, thoroughfare, sidewalk, walkway, footpath or bridlepath, within the territorial jurisdiction of Davao City.

12. Public Parks and Playgrounds – any park, plaza, playground, gym, cemetery, public sports, and passenger water vessels, or other public service or recreational areas created, designated or maintained by the Barangay, City, or National Government as a public area.

13. Restaurant – refers to any establishment offering meals and/or refreshments to the public for a fee; and

14. Other Public Areas – the following shall be considered as "other public areas" within the meaning of this Ordinance:
o Private areas generally open to the public, such as parking areas, green spaces, gardens, playgrounds, and other areas for recreational and leisure activities; and
o Uninhabited private places used without the permission of the owner such as beaches, empty lots, abandoned houses, or buildings.

ARTICLE II. REGULATIONS ON SIGNAGES

SECTION 4. PRESCRIBED SIGNS – Every establishment supplying, selling, serving, and/or offering liquor and other alcoholic beverages shall be required to put up a prescribed sign bearing the words "NO LIQUOR TO MINORS," "LIQUOR LAST CALL BY 12 A.M." and "NO LIQUOR BY 10:00 A.M." the letters of which shall not be less than three (3) inches tall and be placed in conspicuous areas within the establishment.

In every public area, such as public parks and public playgrounds, there shall be visible and well-maintained "NO LIQUOR" signs with at least eight by eighteen (8x18) inches in size and the International "NO ALCOHOL" symbol (consisting of a pictorial representation of a liquor bottle and alcoholic beverage enclosed in a red circle with a red border). The remaining forty percent (40%) of the sign shall show the percentage of said signage. The remaining sixty percent (60%) of the sign shall show the pertinent information/precautionary statement, as follows:

ARTICLE III. REGULATIONS ON THE SALE AND DISTRIBUTION
SECTION 5. PROHIBITION ON THE SELLING, SERVING, PROVIDING AND OFFERING OF LIQUOR AND OTHER ALCOHOLIC BEVERAGES TO MINORS.
A. No person or establishment may sell, serve, provide, or offer liquor, alcoholic beverages, coconut wine or tuba, and other locally distilled alcoholic drinks to minors. Likewise, it shall be unlawful for any person to practice, or establishment to allow the "tagay system" or sharing of glasses and to have a drinking session with minors, regardless of the relationship of the minor to the person with them.
B. When the age of a person to be served is in doubt, the owners, managers, or employees of said establishments are mandated to require the presentation of such person's valid government-issued identification (ID) card, which shall be "prima facie" evidence of the age of said persons.
a. Persons who fail to show their valid government-issued ID cards or who present such ID which is of questionable validity shall be denied service. In the case of minors, he/she shall be immediately referred to the City Social Welfare and Development Office (CSWDO) for his/her rescue. The children rescued under this Ordinance shall be subjected to and dealt with following the provisions of the Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act of 2006 under R.A. No. 9344, as amended by R.A. No. 10630, and the Child and Youth Welfare Code of 1974, under Presidential Decree (P.D.) No. 603.
b. Persons or establishments who fail to require the presentation of such valid government-issued ID cards shall be held liable under Section 15 of this Ordinance.
c. It shall not be a defense for any person, or establishment and its employees in selling, serving, providing, or offering any liquor and other alcoholic beverages that he/she/it did not know or was not aware of the real age of the minor. Neither shall it be a defense that he/she/it did not know nor had any reason to believe that any liquor and alcoholic beverages were for the utilization or consumption of the minor to whom it was sold.
C. Persons who acquire, buy, purchase or possess liquor and other alcoholic beverages on behalf of a minor, or direct, order, compel, request, ask, force, abet, or permit a minor to acquire, buy, purchase, possess, use or consume liquor and other alcoholic beverages shall be accordingly penalized under this Ordinance.

SECTION 6. PROHIBITION ON THE SELLING OF LIQUOR: - Any person or establishment selling liquor, alcoholic beverages, coconut wine or tuba, and other locally-distilled alcoholic drinks and the like, that causes intoxication, are hereby prohibited from selling the same beginning one o'clock in the morning (1:00 A.M.) until eight o'clock in the morning (8:00 A.M.).

SECTION 7. PROHIBITION ON THE CONSUMPTION OF LIQUOR WITHIN A COMMERCIAL ESTABLISHMENT: - All establishments that allow the drinking of liquor, alcoholic beverages, coconut wine or tuba, and other locally-distilled alcoholic drinks and the like within its premises shall also be prohibited from allowing the consumption of the same within such premises beginning one o'clock in the morning (1:00 A.M.) until two o'clock in the afternoon (2:00 P.M.). Likewise, all establishments shall be subjected to the prohibition provided under Section 6 of this Ordinance.

In establishments, the last call or last order of liquor or any other alcoholic beverages must be done by twelve midnight (12:00 A.M.) and all orders shall be consumed by one o'clock in the morning (1:00 A.M.). Posting of such signs shall also be visibly placed in the establishment as provided in Section 4 of this Ordinance.

SECTION 8. REGULATION ON CONSUMPTION OF LIQUOR IN ESTABLISHMENTS NEAR SCHOOLS, HEALTH FACILITIES, PLACES OF WORSHIP, FACILITIES FREQUENTED BY MINORS AND PUBLIC BUILDINGS: - All establishments located within a distance of fifty (50) meters from the perimeter of any schools, health facilities, places of worship, facilities frequented by minors and public buildings, shall not be allowed to serve or offer liquor or other alcoholic beverages beginning one o'clock in the morning (1:00 A.M.) to six o'clock in the evening (6:00 P.M.). Likewise, all establishments shall be subjected to the prohibition provided under Section 6 of this Ordinance.

SECTION 9. SPECIAL EXEMPTIONS FOR TOURISM ESTABLISHMENTS: - Accommodation establishments, such as hotels and resorts, that have been certified by the Department of Tourism (DOT) and/or the City Tourism Operations Office (CTOO) as accredited tourism-accredited accommodation establishments, shall be exempt from the prohibitions provided under Sections 7 and 8 of this Ordinance; provided that, alcoholic beverages shall not be served or offered to persons under the influence of intoxication; provided that, the person-in-charge of such accommodation establishment shall undertake and provide measures to suppress disorderly conduct and disturbance of the place and its surroundings due to Liquor use or consumption; Provided, further, that all accommodation establishments referred herein shall be subjected to the prohibition provided under Section 6 of this Ordinance.

SECTION 10. PROHIBITION ON CONSUMPTION IN PUBLIC PLACES: - The consumption of alcoholic beverages in public streets, sidewalks, public parks, playgrounds, and other public areas is prohibited twenty-four (24) hours and seven (7) days a week, subject to the provisions of the succeeding sections hereunder.

The consumption of the same aboard any motor vehicle found or parked within the prohibited areas also constitutes a violation of this Ordinance.

SECTION 11. EXEMPTIONS TO CONSUMPTION IN PUBLIC PLACES: - The following shall be exempted from the provisions of Section 10 of this Ordinance:

a. Customers of any establishment whose business is authorized or permitted by the City to use a portion or part of the street, sidewalk, or public park; and

b. Special occasions or festivities that shall be authorized or permitted by the City or through a Barangay Resolution from the Barangay Council concerned.

**SECTION 12. SPECIAL PERMIT.** - Any person or establishment selling, serving, or offering liquor or any other alcoholic beverages within City-owned or Barangay or City-maintained establishments, Plazas, Gyms, or Streets, shall only be allowed through special events: provided that, a Special Permit from the City Mayor's Office has been issued in his/her favor allowing the selling, serving, or offering of the same during such special event held at a certain location mentioned herein on a specific date and time.

**SECTION 13. LIQUORS OF DOMESTIC MANUFACTURE: WHEN SALE OF, MAY BE PROHIBITED.** - Whenever a domestically-manufactured or adulterated liquor or alcoholic beverage is found by the Anti-Vices Task Force (AVTF) to be unlicensed before the FDA, the same must be reported to FDA for proper action. Once the FDA issues an order, report, or advisory against any unlicensed domestically-manufactured or adulterated liquor or alcoholic beverages, AVTF shall ensure that the Business Bureau prohibits the manufacturing and/or sale of the same, revocation of the Business Permit, and closure of the establishment.

In case an unlicensed domestically manufactured or adulterated liquor or alcoholic beverage is discovered by the CHO to be IDLH to a person after conducting laboratory tests, the same must be reported to the FDA for proper action and confirmation. When the FDA issues a determination that the unlicensed domestically-manufactured or adulterated liquor or alcoholic beverage is IDLH, the CHO shall immediately refer the matter to the Business Bureau for purposes of prohibiting the manufacturing and/or selling of the same, revocation of the Business Permit, and to order the closure of the establishment.

Any such adulteration made or caused to be made by the owner, or operator of the establishment shall be penalized under this Ordinance.

In the case of minors, the rescued children shall be turned over to the CSWDO who shall meet with the child and his/her parents or legal guardians for proper alcohol cessation counseling and the development of the appropriate diversion and rehabilitation program, in coordination with the Barangay Council for the Protection of Children (BCPC) and the CHQ, and they shall be dealt with the provisions of the Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act of 2006 and the Child and Youth Welfare Code of 1974.

In case of a single proprietorship business, the owner of the offending establishment shall be made liable for violating this Ordinance.

If the violation is committed by a corporation, trust, firm, partnership, association, or any other juridical person, the penalty shall be imposed upon the President, General Manager, and other guilty officer or officers of such corporation, trust, firm, partnership, association, or entity.

For public conveyances, the operator, driver, and/or conductor shall also be made liable for the violation of this Ordinance. In the absence of evidence to the contrary, the manager and/or operator shall be presumed to be the owner of the business under this Ordinance without prejudice to the application of other laws and regulations.

Every penalty imposed for the commission of a violation under this Ordinance shall carry with it the forfeiture of the proceeds of the crime and the instruments or tools with which it was committed.

Such proceeds and instruments or tools shall be confiscated and forfeited in favor of the City Government of Davao unless they are the property of a third person not liable for the offense, but those articles which are not subject to lawful commerce shall be destroyed.

In addition to the penalty imposed for the violation of any provision of this Ordinance, the same shall carry with it the confiscation and forfeiture of all alcoholic beverages involved in the commission of the offense.

SECTION 16. ALCOHOL CESSION COUNSELING: - Alcohol cessation counseling in accredited clinics and district health centers is required after the administrative fine has been paid. The person who violates this Ordinance and the violator has undergone counseling in signing the citation ticket and attaching the required counseling form. After complying with the said requirements, the violator shall return the citation ticket to the apprehending officer or VRU if the deputized enforcer is not a member of the DCPO.

Notwithstanding the payment of the administrative fine herein provided, the failure of any person violating any of the provisions of this Ordinance to undergo the alcohol cessation counseling shall still warrant the filing of case in court.

SECTION 17. CITATION TICKET: A citation ticket shall be issued to violators of the provisions of this Ordinance. The citation ticket shall state, among others, the name and address of the violator, the specific violation committed, and the corresponding administrative penalty. The citation ticket shall be given to the violator, and a copy shall be endorsed to VRU, the second copy shall be given to the violator, and the third copy shall be returned by the apprehending enforcer.

Failure to pay any of the administrative fines per count shall result in an additional administrative fine of One Thousand Pesos ($100.00) a day per count; provided that, the total amount of administrative fine per count shall not exceed Five Thousand Pesos ($5,000.00); provided, further, that, non-payment of the total administrative fines per count within three (3) working days from the time of apprehension or issuance of citation ticket or failure to undergo the mandated alcohol cessation counseling shall lead to the prosecution of the case in court subject to the penalties provided herein, this is without prejudice to the prosecution of their persons.

SECTION 18. DEPUTIZED ENforcERS. - The City Mayor, through VRU, may
deputize and authorize representatives from DPCPO to issue the corresponding citation tickets
and apprehend violators, and the same shall be responsible for the strict implementation of
this Ordinance.

Barangay officials or representatives from the PSSO - Civil Security Unit (CSU), the
Business Bureau, CEE, CHQ, CSWDO, private sector, non-government organizations,
and government organizations may also be deputized and authorized to monitor the compliance
with the provisions of this Ordinance and report violations to the proper apprehending officers.

SECTION 19. TRAINING AND DEPUTATION. - All deputized enforcers shall be required to undergo training in enforcement before they are deputized and allowed to enforce
the provisions of this Ordinance. Only those who shall complete and pass the training shall be deputized as
enforcers by the City Mayor.

SECTION 20. BARANGAY OFFICIALS. - Barangay Officials in Davao City shall assist
with the information dissemination on the salient features of this Ordinance, and shall properly coordinate with the Business Bureau, TESDA XI, Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA) XI, the Business Bureau, DPCPO, VRU, PSSO-CSU, CEE, CHQ, and/or CSWDO in strictly enforcing the provisions herein within the respective territorial jurisdiction of their Barangay.

Barangay Officials shall immediately coordinate with DPCPO and/or CSWDO and ensure the rescue of minors whenever they are involved in the possession, sale, acquisition, service,
distribution, consumption of, or, engaged in any transaction involving liquor or any other alcoholic beverages. Such matter must be promptly reported to the Business Bureau for appropriate action in case a certain business establishment is found to be part thereof.

SECTION 21. REVIEW OF LIQUOR ORDINANCE. - VRU shall conduct a review of
the provisions provided in this Ordinance every five (5) years; or whenever the agency deems it necessary to amend the provisions provided in this Ordinance, to ensure the relevance and
appropriateness of the regulations and submit the same to the Sangguniang Panlungsod, for amendment.

SECTION 22. INFORMATION, EDUCATION, AND COMMUNICATION CAMPAIGN. - There shall be a Public Information Drive regarding the implementation of this
Ordinance and it shall be headed by the City Information Office (CIO), with close coordination with DepeD, CHEH, ALS, TESDA, the Business Bureau, DPCPO, VRU, PSSO-CSU, CEE, CHQ, CSWDO and other enforcing and implementing agencies. Information dissemination through signages, flyers, audio-visual presentations, and other forms of public information dissemination shall be established.

The AVTR, in coordination with CHQ, CSWDO, and the Department of Education XI (DepeD-XI) shall undertake information dissemination programs and educational awareness campaigns on the ill effects of all vices to schools, colleges, and universities, conduct capacity-building programs, and establish vices cessation clinics in the City.

SECTION 23. IMPLEMENTING RULES AND REGULATIONS (IRR). - The VRU shall promulgate such rules and regulations, with the approval of the City Mayor, as are necessary to ensure the effective implementation of this Ordinance.

Absence of the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of this Ordinance shall not preclude the enforcement of the provisions of this Ordinance.

ARTICLE V. TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

SECTION 24. FUNDING. - An amount of Five Million Pesos (P5,000,000.00) shall be appropriated by the City Government of Davao in its Annual Budget or in any supplemental budget, as the case may be, to carry out the purposes of this Ordinance.

**SECTION 25. REPEALING CLAUSE:**
Any Ordinance, local issuances, or rules inconsistent with the provisions of this Ordinance are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.

In this regard, Ordinance No. 004-13, Series of 2013, and Ordinance No. 0410-18, Series of 2018, are hereby repealed.

**SECTION 26. SEPARABILITY CLAUSE:**
If, for any reason, any Section of this Ordinance is declared unconstitutional or invalid, the other sections or provisions hereof which are not affected thereby shall continue to be in full force and effect.

**SECTION 27. EFFECTIVITY CLAUSE:**
This Ordinance shall take effect 15 days upon approval, posting, and publication in a newspaper of local circulation.
